# Judged criteria

Rampa runs axe-core first and reports its violations as they are. A judgment module then sends a model only the residue for one WCAG success criterion: what the engine passed on syntax, could not decide, or does not check. Each module lives in `src/criteria/` and does the same six things:

1. **Candidates.** Picks the nodes to judge from the normalized snapshot, never the DOM, and leaves out nodes the engine already failed.
2. **Prompt.** One criterion per prompt, with the normative WCAG text and only the context that criterion needs. Page data sits inside tags the model is told never to obey. A closing tag of its block inside the data (a page that writes `</text>`) is escaped as `<\/text>`, so the page cannot end the block and write outside it; the prompt of a page with no such tag is unchanged, byte for byte.
3. **Schema.** The model answers in JSON with a verdict (`pass`, `fail`, `cannot_tell`), evidence and the fields the criterion needs.
4. **Verification.** Every claim is checked against the snapshot. A claim that does not hold is dropped and counted, never reported. Where the evidence must be an element's text, it must be all of it or most of it in one piece (60% at least), and hold three letters or digits at least unless it is the whole text: one or two ("de") are in nearly any text. A pass that takes away a probe's finding (2.1.1, 2.1.4, 2.2.1, 2.5.1, 3.2.2, 3.3.8) or an item to review (4.1.3) must quote the page by the same rule, from one listed text, never two joined; a 4.1.3 pass without that quote leaves its item for a person, saying the model passed it without evidence.
5. **Message**, in English and Portuguese.
6. **Patch**, when a fix can be written: a changed attribute, a changed text, or a replaced element. A patch is a proposal for a person to review.

Fourteen criteria have a module that `--criteria` selects. Eight run by default; 1.3.1, 1.3.2, 1.3.3, 1.4.5, 2.5.3 and 3.3.1 run when asked for (`--criteria 1.3.1,1.3.2,1.3.3,1.4.5,2.5.3,3.3.1`). Eight more modules, for 2.1.1, 2.1.4, 2.2.1, 2.4.3, 2.5.1, 3.2.2, 3.3.8 and 4.1.3, are not chosen with `--criteria`: each asks a model one question about what its probe recorded, and runs only when that probe ran and left something to ask ([below](#questions-a-probe-leaves-to-a-model)).

**Findings and suggestions (2.4.4, 2.4.6).** For these two criteria a model's verified fail is a finding only when a fact Rampa checks itself supports it (`grounded`): a text on the generic lists ("Section 2", "Field 1", or "Read more" with nothing around the link that could complete it), a link text that names only a file's format ("HTML", "PDF") with nothing in its context that says which document, or only its download ("Download") in a table row whose document is a data cell, a raw address, links that share a text and lead elsewhere from the same context, a link to a part of this page that the page names while the link's text and context name none of it, a numbered heading under no parent that names its subject, or a label several fields share with nothing visible to tell them apart. Any other fail, such as "the heading names a different topic" or "this specific link text says nothing", is reported as the model's suggestion: an item in `needsReview` with the model's name and its suggested text, for a person to decide. It is never a failure, never in the findings or below the threshold, and never changes the exit code; the criterion's coverage counts it as review ("N of them model suggestions with no checked fact behind them") and its status is "needs review". The reason is measured, not assumed: on the real-page study's pages, not one of the model's 2.4.4 and 2.4.6 findings was a real failure (0 of 38 on the dev pages, 0 of 66 decided on the held-out ones), and the ones verification still let through were readings no checked fact supports ([notes-244-246.md](studies/notes-244-246.md)). Gemma 4 12B cannot tell a specific link or heading text that fails from one that passes on real pages, so Rampa does not let it decide that alone. On the ACT test cases this reports more at the default threshold than before (links with one name in one context, shared labels), and the purpose-built failures no fact supports ("Weather" over opening hours, "Menu" on a first-name field) are suggestions.

A module may also decide a candidate without a model (`decide`), from facts of the snapshot: then no model is asked, and in the coverage that decision is a method of kind `rule` (`decidedBy`), not a judgment. 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 do so with a language identifier, 2.4.2 for a home page titled with its site's name, and 1.3.3 for an instruction whose target something else names.

**Long pages.** On each page, a criterion sends the model at most 50 candidates (`--max-candidates`, or `maxCandidates` in the config; 0 for no cap; 10 with `--fast`, see [performance.md](performance.md)): those a checked fact could make a finding of first (2.4.4 and 2.4.6: a text on the generic lists, such as "Read more", a format or a download with nothing around it, an address, a link text shared with links that lead elsewhere, a link to a part of the page that the page names and the link's text and context do not, a numbered heading under no subject, a shared label with nothing to tell its fields apart; the other criteria: those whose text is on the generic lists), then the rest in page order. What a criterion decides without a model never counts. `--time-limit <seconds>` stops asking the model that long after a page starts loading (in a crawl, after its turn to be judged starts); a call under way is given up, cached judgments are still read, and the report has what was collected and judged until then. Candidates left out either way are counted in the criterion's summary (`capped`, `timedOut`) and in its coverage (`notJudged` on the judgment method, which leaves a criterion with no failure as "needs review", even when an engine rule found nothing to fail), and a note says which. The real-page study lost four of its twelve dev pages, all long listings and references, to a 900 s timeout that left no report.

**Names as the browser computes them.** On the web, the collector also reads Chromium's own accessibility tree (see [rules.md](rules.md#browser-tree-rules-412-253-131-and-413)). 2.4.4, 2.4.6 and 3.3.2, whose subject is an accessible name, judge each link, heading and field by the name the browser computes. Text hidden from assistive technology is left out (a table of contents number), an image's alt is in, and a heading that is only an image is named by its alt. The other criteria read the collector's own names. On the ACT sets this changed one prompt: a new 2.4.6 candidate, judged a pass, with the same scores. On real pages it changes many, as rules.md shows.

**Why 1.4.5 is not on by default.** OCR now passes most pictures without a model (on the real-page study's dev pages, 96 of 121), so the cost that kept it off is gone, but its precision on real pages is not measured: the coverage plan's gate (EVAL-1) asks for 35 findings reviewed by a person with no false positive, and those pages gave 3. Whether a banner that pairs a product photo with a headline is an image of text is also a call people still disagree on.

The evaluation numbers below are from one run of Gemma 4 12B on a local GPU (Ollama, reasoning off) on 2026-10-09, ACT test cases `a9a1483e`. The prompts of 1.3.5, 1.4.5 and 3.3.2 were revised after reading the errors of earlier runs on these same cases, without copying test pages into the prompts, so read the numbers as a working pipeline, not a result. [models.md](models.md#four-local-models-measured) runs the same evaluation with three other local models (Qwen3-VL 8B, Qwen 3.5 9B, Ministral 3 14B), criterion by criterion. [benchmark.md](benchmark.md) runs other accessibility checkers (axe-core, Pa11y, IBM Equal Access, Siteimprove Alfa, QualWeb) on the same ACT test cases and corrupted pairs, scored the same way, criterion by criterion.

## 1.1.1 Non-text Content

- **axe-core checks** that an image has a text alternative. It passes `alt="img-1"` on a photo of a dog.
- **Rampa judges** every image with a non-empty alternative that screen readers read (`img`, `input type="image"`, `role="img"`, a named `canvas`): whether the alternative serves the same purpose as the image here. It asks another question about images hidden from assistive technology (below).
- **Context:** the image as it renders (an element screenshot, with fixed and sticky layers such as cookie banners hidden while it is taken), the current alternative, the file name, the name of the link or button it sits in, nearby text, and the language of the image (its element's, else the page's) for the description and the suggestion.
- **Capture:** each image is scrolled into view and given up to 3 s (15 s per page) to load and decode, so a lazy image (`loading="lazy"`, a `data-src` swap) is captured, not its placeholder. When other images share its box, as carousel slides stacked in one place do, they are hidden and this one is made visible while it is captured. An image is left without a capture, and so is not judged, when the screenshot would not be its own pixels: most of it outside the page (an off-canvas menu), cut off by the box that holds it, transparent, still loading or still a placeholder, blank (one flat color, the same as without it), or stacked and captured the same as another picture. The snapshot records why on the node (`native.imageSkipped`), and the report sums it up in a note. These came from the real-page study: the stacked slides of www.ufc.br were all judged on slide 1's crop, and the drauziovarella.uol.com.br logo at x = -152 on a slice of another bar.
- **Functional images:** an image that is all a link or button holds (no other text or named image, and no `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` on the control), and an image button (`input type="image"`), names its control, so the prompt says its alternative should say where the link leads or what the button does, not describe the picture: a pictogram or a logo need not depict what it names (techniques H30 and H36). The prompt adds the control's `title`, the address and, when Rampa read it for 2.4.4, the destination's title and heading, and the visible text next to the control. A fail that says the alternative "describes something the image does not show", or leaves out what the image conveys (which, framed this way, the model uses for "does not say where the link leads"), is dropped when the alternative, without words such as "ícone", "icon", "logo" or "imagem", has only words of the control's `title`, the destination's title or the text next to it, or holds all of theirs. A fail that stays says the alternative does not say where the link leads. From the real-page study's dev split: twelve "ÍCONE - …" quick-access links on www.ufc.br, each titled like its alternative, and "Meu INSS - Central de Serviços" on gov.br, captioned the same. The same claim is also dropped when the alternative names the destination another way, from facts Rampa computes and never puts in the prompt: it holds every word of a part of the destination's title or heading that is not the site's own name (the parts of the page's own title, and parts read at two addresses or more, such as "Portal Unicamp"); it holds every word of a part of the link's address, the last segment of its path or a label of another host, also when that part glues the alternative's words together ("novasaude", "museudavida"); its own words, the site's name aside, are at least half of the words of such a part ("ANBIlogo" and `/anbi-information`, but not "Brasil" and a slug of seven words); or it names a service such as X, Facebook or YouTube and the link leads to that service's host, or leads to no service's host (a share script's `#`) and sits in a short row whose text names two other services (medlineplus.gov's "X" between "Facebook" and "Pinterest"; an "X" that closes a dialog names nothing). None of these facts, nor the ones above, speaks for an alternative the placeholder pattern or the generic-word list flags. From the fresh-page study's dev split ([notes](studies/notes-111-dev-fixes.md)).
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the current alternative; the model must say what the image shows; a fail needs a problem and, unless the image is decorative, a suggested alternative under 250 characters that differs from the current one. A fail is dropped when its suggestion is a label rather than a description ("decorative", "image", "photo", "icon", "none" and their Portuguese and Spanish forms), or only removes or reorders words of the current alternative, as when "ÍCONE - Ônibus da UFC" became "Ônibus da UFC" in the real-page study. A word glued to the kind of picture or to "link" counts as two ("Instagramlogo" is "Instagram" and "logo"), so the suggestion "Instagram" only rewords it, while "Facebook" does not. Two more facts refute a claim that the alternative describes something the image does not show: the image is not all its link or button holds and its alternative is the control's visible text, or a run of words of it (ce.gov.br's service pictograms, named like the service the link shows: redundant at worst, as technique H2 says, which also covers a claim that it leaves out what the image conveys); and the model's own answer keeps every word the alternative names, in its suggestion ("Lula" became "Lula com um selo de 16%", "Pinacoteca" became "Pinacoteca de São Paulo") or in its suggestion and its description of the image together, so it still names it. The model writes both in the language Rampa asks for, the image's element's or else the page's, taken to be the alternative's (version 5; before, the description was always in English, so a Portuguese alternative's words could never meet it); a name or a number reads the same in any language, so it still meets a description a model writes in English anyway. On the fresh-page study's dev pages and the ACT cases, the check on the description dropped no claim, and only tests back it: the two dev false positives version 5 removed, "Capa da edição 751" and "Boletim InfoGripe", were dropped by the check on the suggestion, because the new prompt's suggestions kept their words ([notes](studies/notes-remaining-errors.md)). A claim that the alternative leaves out what the image conveys is not dropped for keeping it: "Chart" can rightly become "Chart of sales by quarter". File names and placeholders such as `IMG_2034.jpg` are recognized by a pattern, which wins over the model's label in the message; an alternative equal to its file's name counts only when that name looks like one, with no space and a separator or a digit (`Tarja-Especiais-Site-Interna-1.png`), not when the file is named in words (`Lula.png` for a photo of Lula).
- **Patch:** sets `alt` (or `aria-label` on elements that take no `alt`) to the suggestion, or to empty for a decorative image.
- **Images hidden from assistive technology** (ACT e88epe, experimental): an `img` with `alt=""`, an `img`, `svg` or `canvas` with `role="presentation"` or `"none"` or under `aria-hidden="true"`, an `svg` with no name (no `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, `title` attribute or `<title>` child) and no role, and a `canvas` with no name and no role. Screen reader users get nothing of these, which is right for decoration. Not one inside an element its author named (`aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`), whose name stands for it, nor one off the page or not rendered.
  - **Cheap signals first.** No model looks at a hidden image that is icon-sized (32 px or less on both sides, or under 16 px on one), the same picture as two others on the page (the same address, or the same svg drawing) or as an image that has a name, inside a link or button (a named one says what it does; an unnamed one is axe-core's to report), or next to text that already holds the words the page calls it by (its file name, `title`, `<title>` or unread `alt`). Of those left, the first 10 of a page are judged.
  - **Capture:** an `img` is among the images already; an `svg` or a `canvas` is cropped as it renders, after them, with the same scroll, wait and skip reasons, so one that is blank or cut off is noted, not judged.
  - **The question:** with the picture and the text screen readers get right before and after it (300 characters each way, visually hidden text included, `aria-hidden` text left out), whether the image is decoration or the page relies on it for information that text does not give: words drawn in it as its message, a logo, a chart, a diagram, a map, a symbol that stands alone. A pattern (even of letters or logos) and a photo that illustrates the topic or sets a mood are decorative, even when it shows a person or a place the text does not name; words on things in a photo are part of the scene. The model says what it shows and copies the words drawn in it.
  - **Verification:** a fail needs a description, the information and a suggested alternative that is not a label. It is dropped when more than half the words of that information, or of the suggestion, are in the text around the image, and when the words drawn in the image all are. A word is there in another form ("logos", "owners" for "own"), or as initials of words in a row ("NP" beside "Nothing Personal"), and what the claim quotes of that text does not count as information.
  - **Message and patch:** says how the image is hidden (`alt=""`, `role="none"`, `aria-hidden="true"`, an svg or a canvas with no name) and what it conveys. `alt` for an `img` with `alt=""`, `aria-label` for an unnamed `svg`; an `img` hidden by its own `role` or `aria-hidden` gets its start tag rewritten, for review. A fail stays below the default confidence threshold until the check passes the evaluation gate: `--verbose` lists it, and `--min-confidence low` reports it as a finding that fails the build.
- **Evaluation** (Gemma 4 12B, 2026-10-09): 23a2a8 precision 1.00 and recall 1.00 (n = 18), qt1vmo 1.00 and 1.00 (n = 16), the same as before the hidden images; e88epe 1.00 and 1.00 (n = 20, 5 of 5 failed examples, axe-core 0 of 5); pairs 2 of 2. Each ACT set is scored by the question that implements its rule: 23a2a8's Inapplicable Example 3, a logo with `aria-hidden="true"` and no `alt`, is e88epe's Failed Example 2 too, and the hidden-image question reports it, which counts for e88epe and not against 23a2a8. The hidden-image prompt was revised after reading these cases: the first run failed a celebration photo marked `role="none"` next to the greeting it illustrates (precision 0.83), read as "a particular place the text does not name". Prompt and verification were revised again after reading www.mozilla.org's errors (below). The verification facts from the fresh-page study's dev split (2026-10-15) changed no prompt: the same run, with all 32 answers read from its cache, gives the same numbers, and they drop no claim on these cases. Version 5 (2026-10-23), which asks for the description in the alternative's language, gave every one of its 56 evaluated pages, ACT cases and pairs, the same outcome, with no claim dropped ([notes](studies/notes-remaining-errors.md)).
- **On real pages:** www.mozilla.org has 10 hidden images; the signals leave 8 (gallery photos and footer logos with `alt=""`). Verification drops 6 (the footer logos beside "Mozilla", a product mark beside its name, a slogan on shirts whose words the text around holds, a pattern of a magazine's initials under its name), one passes, and one stays, below the threshold: a photo of a speaker whose stage backdrop the model read as words the page gives. www.ufc.br has 16 hidden images, all icon-sized or inside links, and www.gov.br/pt-br one, inside a link: no call.
- **Pictures that change:** the judgment sees each image once, as the page loaded. A carousel or a script that changes the picture while its name stays the same (F20) is the state-variants probe's (`--probe variants`, rule `rampa/carousel-name`, [probes](probes.md#state-variants-111-and-311---probe-variants-rules-rampacarousel-name-and-rampalanguage-versions)).
- **Limits:** a suggested alternative cannot be checked against the page, since only the model sees the pixels. A hidden image the cheap signals call decorative is never looked at, so an informative icon with `alt=""` and no text next to it is missed, and so are hidden images past the tenth of a page. Product photos and portraits are left to the model's reading of "illustrates the topic", and words on things in a photo are still sometimes taken for information. At most 25 images per page are captured, in document order; the rest are noted as over the limit. What clips an image without an overflowing box (`clip-path`, a mask, an element painted over it that is not a fixed layer or another image) is not detected, and the capture shows it as the page does.

## 1.3.1 Info and Relationships

Experimental, on request (`--criteria 1.3.1`): every failure is reported at low confidence, below the default threshold. The structure rules for 1.3.1 (tables, labels tied to nothing; [rules.md](rules.md#structure-rules-131-and-412)) run without it.

- **axe-core checks** lists, definition lists, ARIA parent and child roles and table header references. It does not see text styled as a heading; its experimental `p-as-heading`, which compares a paragraph with the ones around it, was wrong on every real page in rules.md, so Rampa does not run it.
- **What it looks for:** failure [F2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F2), text that looks like a heading and is not marked up as one: sighted people see one more heading, and screen reader users cannot find it in the list of headings or jump to it.
- **The facts:** the collector records the typography of each heading and of each short block of text that could pass for one (`native.look`: size to half a pixel, weight, first font family, style, case and color), and the look most of the page's body text has (`native.bodyLook` on the root). A short block is a block element (`p`, `div`, a `span` or `strong` laid out as a block...) of up to 120 characters and 15 words, with no link, control, picture or block inside it, not inside a heading, a control, a label or a caption, whose text sits in one element (`<p><strong>Returns</strong></p>` reads the `strong`).
- **Candidates:** a block whose look is exactly that of a heading the page marks up, when that look stands out from the body text (two pixels larger, or bold where the body is not), and when content follows it before the next heading or the next such text: 40 characters or more, fields and named pictures included. Left out: text in a list item, a term, a cell, a caption, a label or a legend, whose own markup says what it is; text with fewer than two letters (prices, statistics such as "1,9%"); a sentence of six words or more ending in a stop, a question mark or a colon (a lead-in to a list); a line right under a heading, with nothing between, which is its subtitle or its lead. At most 15 a page.
- **The question:** with the headings it looks like and the content after it, whether the text works as a heading that names that content, or is something else: a label for one value, a price, a slogan, a call to action, a caption, a name in a list.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the text; a fail must say what the content after it is about, and that content must exist.
- **Message and patch:** names the headings it looks like and the look ("24 px, weight 700, georgia"), and asks for `<hN>` at their most common level. The patch adds `role="heading"` and `aria-level` to the start tag, the change a start tag can hold.
- **Evaluation:** no ACT rule covers F2. Pairs (`heading-as-text`) take b49b2e's passing pages, each a heading over a paragraph, and add a section whose title is a `div` styled like the page's first heading, with a paragraph after it: 3 of 3 with Gemma 4 12B (2026-10-11), against 0 for axe-core. The other three passing pages have no `h1` to `h6` with text to copy (a `span` with `role="heading"`, one moved off the page, an `h1` that is only an image), so they make no pair.
- **On real pages** (2026-10-11, Gemma 4 12B, `--min-confidence low`): a first run reported two failures, both wrong. On www.gov.br, "Encontre o serviço ou a informação desejada pelas opções abaixo:", a lead-in sentence right under the h2 "Navegue por categoria", styled like another of the page's h2s; on www.gov.uk/register-to-vote, "Part of", a bold label over one link ("Vote in UK elections"). The model called both headings. Both are now left out by facts, not by the model: a sentence ending in a colon, a line right under a heading, less than 40 characters after. A second run reports nothing on either page, and nothing on rampa.guilhermebs.com.br, whose three platform names in a list ("Web", "Android", "iOS") were candidates until list items were left out. Scanned without a model, www.mozilla.org, www.ibge.gov.br, webaim.org, www.ufc.br, www.bbc.com/news, www.nhs.uk, docs.python.org, www.sosma.org.br and www.usa.gov give no candidate. These rules were written after reading those pages, so the second run is not a held-out measure; no true F2 failure has been seen on a real page yet.
- **Limits:** a heading styled unlike any marked-up heading is not seen, nor one on a page with no heading at all, nor one followed directly by a heading (a box title over a subheading). Card titles inside list items are left out. Looks are compared exactly, so a color or a size that differs by a pixel tells them apart.

## 1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence

- **What it reads:** only what the reading-order rules ([rules](rules.md#reading-order-132)) nominated and could not verify: a group of boxes CSS shows in another order than the markup's (`order`, a reversed flex direction, grid placement, absolute positions) with no numbered steps, heading or split sentence to check, and preformatted text in two columns of short items. Groups of more than 16 boxes are left out. The model never sees anything the rules did not nominate, and never judges what they failed.
- **Rampa asks** whether reading the boxes in the markup's order changes what the content means, compared with the order on screen (or, for the preformatted text, whether reading it line by line changes it): steps out of order, a heading or a label with the wrong content, a sentence split or reversed, items in the wrong group, values that lose their header. Boxes that stand on their own (cards, menu items, a sidebar beside an article, a badge) pass.
- **Context:** the boxes' texts in the markup's order, as a screen reader reads them, and line by line as they show (top to bottom, left to right), with what reorders them; for preformatted text, its lines and the same lines cut into their two columns.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the text of a listed box or line. A fail needs a relation (sequence, heading, sentence, grouping or table), and the box it quotes must be one the markup holds out of its place on screen (one of a pair the markup and the screen order differently, in lines or in columns). A pass names no relation; it may quote one box or list several.
- **Experimental:** every finding is capped at low confidence, below the default threshold, and the module is not a default. It only keeps candidates; it has no patch.
- **Evaluation:** no ACT rule covers 1.3.2. On 2026-10-11 with Gemma 4 12B (Ollama, one run): the five groups the Bootstrap flex utilities page nominates (its header, the reversed and reordered "Flex item" demos) all passed, and so did the five candidates of the near-miss page `test/fixtures/rules/reading-order-controls.html` (a reversed menu, a sidebar moved first, a kicker above its heading, a corner badge, labels with their values). The first run failed one more there, numbered steps the markup holds in order and the screen shows right to left: such a group is no longer a candidate, since the sequence a screen reader reads is the right one. A recipe in two grid columns whose markup interleaves the halves of each column's sentences ("Then fold in the flour", "Whisk the eggs with sugar", "and bake for 30 minutes"…) was kept, quoting "Then fold in the flour", below the threshold.
- **Limits:** whether an order matters is the model's reading of short texts; it sees no layout beyond the lines, no styles and no pictures. Bidirectional text, magazine and dashboard layouts stay manual.

## 1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics

Experimental, on request (`--criteria 1.3.3`): every failure is reported at low confidence, below the default threshold (`--verbose` lists it; `--min-confidence low` reports it as a finding that fails the build).

- **axe-core checks** nothing for 1.3.3.
- **What it looks for:** an instruction that points to something on the page only by where it is, its shape, its size, its orientation or a sound: "Find the menu on the right", "Click the round button", "Tap the star", "Use o menu à esquerda", "Pulse el botón grande". It follows ACT rule [9bd38c](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/9bd38c/proposed/) (proposed): a visual reference word passes when something else identifies the same content, when the word has a non-sensory meaning, when it is in the content's visible text or accessible name, or when the text gives no instruction. ACT's color words are left to 1.4.1, whose color probe reads them (`--probe color`); sound words are in, as WCAG names sound.
- **A failure needs three things to agree.**
  1. **The broad matcher** (`src/rules/words/sensory.ts`, written for Rampa) nominates a sentence with an instruction word ("click", "find", "use", "clique", "veja", "pulse"...) and a sensory phrase, in English, Portuguese or Spanish, without case or accents: a place ("on the right", "below", "à esquerda", "abaixo", "a la derecha"), a shape ("round", "star", "arrow", "estrela", "seta", "lupa"), a size or an orientation next to a noun for what it describes ("the big button", "o botão grande", "tilted text"), a sound ("when you hear the beep", "o bipe"). It leaves out idioms ("above all", "abaixo-assinado"), quantities ("above 18", "acima de 10"), and arrows in a sentence about keys ("the up and down arrows"). Text in another language is not read.
  2. **The snapshot.** Rampa looks for what the sentence points to, where it says: a thing of the kind its noun names (a button, a link, a menu or a list of links, a field, an image, an icon...) in that direction from the sentence's box; something drawn as the shape (a character such as ★ or ▲, a word in its class, id, picture file name or svg title such as `fa-star`, or corners that make it a circle, which the collector measures as `native.shape`); the one of its kind that stands out by size (1.5 times the next); something the collector measured as turned; audio or video for a sound. Then for what else names it: a name the sentence quotes ('labelled "howdy"', 'the "Examples" heading'), a word of its name or text in the sentence, the sensory word in its own text or name ("Round button"), a heading that holds it or comes right before it and shares a word with the sentence, a landmark the sentence names when it is the only one of its role (or a named landmark whose name the sentence uses), the sentence itself ("this tilted text"), and the reading order for "below" and "above": when the next (or previous) thing of the kind is the one meant, the word says where in the text, which is not sensory. Something names it: a pass, decided without a model (`rampa/sensory-words` in the coverage). Nothing found, or nothing in particular to look for ("the column on the right", "the options below:"): no failure could be verified, and the sentence is not a candidate.
  3. **The model** reads the sentence with what was found and says whether it is an instruction that identifies that thing only by those words. A fail is kept when the model says it is an instruction, the words it names are in the sentence and are the matcher's sensory words, and the facts of step 2 hold.
- **Message:** quotes the instruction, the sensory words and what Rampa found ("a list of 2 links "March April", left of the text"), and asks to name the thing in the instruction too, or to give it a heading. No patch: the fix is a sentence to rewrite.
- **Evaluation** (Gemma 4 12B, 2026-10-11, ACT test cases `a9a1483e`): 9bd38c precision 1.00 and recall 0.75 (n = 21; 3 of its 4 failed examples). The miss is Failed Example 3, whose "triangle menu" is on another page: Rampa does not follow the link, and nothing on the page looks like a triangle. 9bd38c is a proposed rule and a sanity check here, not a gate. Pairs (`sensory-only`, which takes from 9bd38c's passing pages what names the target: a quoted label, headings, also in frames, navigation landmarks, a sensory word shown on the control): 5 of 6. The miss is Passed Example 11 corrupted, whose instruction is moved off the page and whose button then sits in the left half of the page, so "on the right" is not true of it. Most of the passing examples are decided without a model: the quoted "howdy", the "Menu" heading, the only navigation landmark, "Round button", the reading order of "below", "this tilted text", the "Examples" heading.
- **On real pages** (2026-10-11, Gemma 4 12B, `--min-confidence low`): no failure, and no sentence was sent to the model. On rampa.guilhermebs.com.br three sentences matched ("open source under the MIT license", "report below the default threshold") and named nothing to look for. On www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/instructions/ ("In the example below, form instructions indicate…") and www.sosma.org.br/contato ("Preencha os campos abaixo…") the reading order passed "below" and "abaixo" without a model. Help pages that point to another application's screen ("At the top left, click Security & sign-in", "No canto superior esquerdo, clique em…" on support.google.com) name no kind of thing on the page, so nothing is looked for. Real failures are rare, and the gate's 35 reviewed findings will need a targeted corpus.
- **Limits:** the identification checks are lenient on purpose (a shared word, a heading next to the thing), so an instruction that only seems to name its target passes. A target on another page, or behind a link, is not followed. Directions are read from boxes at one viewport: "on the right" means right of the sentence and in the right half of the page. Size words need two or more things of the kind to compare; color words are 1.4.1's; a sound cannot be checked beyond audio or video being on the page. Instructions in images and media are not read.

## 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose

- **axe-core checks** that an `autocomplete` value is a valid token for the input type (ACT 73f2c2). It says nothing when the attribute is missing, or when the token is valid but names other data, such as a phone field with `autocomplete="email"` (failure F107).
- **Rampa judges** every text-like field that takes input (text, email, tel, url, password, number, date, month, `select`, `textarea`; not search boxes, buttons, checkboxes, hidden, disabled or read-only fields, nor spam honeypots, which are fields out of sight, off screen or squeezed to a pixel, that are out of the tab order (`tabindex="-1"`) or named like a trap such as "honeypot", "hp" or "website"): whose information it collects (the user's, someone else's, nobody's), which of the 53 WCAG input purposes it asks for, and whether its `autocomplete` names that purpose.
- **Context:** the label (or the placeholder when there is none), the input type, `name` and `id`, a select's first options, what the current `autocomplete` value means, the heading above, the fieldset legend, the other fields in the form (a "Name" next to "Card number" is the name on the card) and the form's buttons. The prompt lists every token with its meaning, because local models never read the JSON schema.
- **Chromium's form issues:** the collector reads the issues Chromium raises on its own form check (the Audits domain of the DevTools protocol, as in the Issues panel): an empty `autocomplete`, a token that is wrong but well intended (`telephone`), and a token written in `name` or `id` with no `autocomplete`. An issue that still holds for the field as collected goes into the prompt as corroboration, and the message of a fail says that Chromium flags the field too. It never makes a finding by itself: Chromium also flags valid tokens it does not fill, such as `nickname`.
- **Someone else's data:** when the label, placeholder, legend or heading has a word for another person ("recipient", "gift", "emergency contact", "destinatário", "cônjuge", "beneficiario"), a fail stays below the confidence threshold, since whose data a field holds is the call the model most often gets wrong.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the label or placeholder. A fail must be about the user's own data and name a purpose that is in the WCAG list (not `one-time-code`), that is not a transaction amount or currency (the Understanding document notes those are rarely about the user), and that axe-core's own type table allows on that input, so the patch can never break the engine rule. A mismatch claim is dropped when the current token already names that purpose or a close one (`tel` and `tel-national`, `email` and `username`, `new-password` and `current-password`). A language or country selector with no other field around it is a page switcher, not data entry, and is dropped.
- **Patch:** adds or replaces `autocomplete`, keeping a `section-*` prefix and `billing` or `shipping`.
- **Evaluation:** ACT 73f2c2 measures the engine; with judgment, precision drops to 0.83 because two of its inapplicable cases, a "Username" field with `autocomplete=""`, are 1.3.5 failures that Rampa reports. No ACT rule judges missing or mismatched tokens, so pairs come from 73f2c2's passing pages, with the token removed (`autocomplete-drop`) or swapped for a valid token for unrelated data (`autocomplete-swap`): 8 of 9 and 6 of 7. The miss in each is "Partner's email address", someone else's data, where passing is right.
- **Limits:** whether data is about the user is the model's call (a gift recipient, a friend). A field that combines two purposes, such as "email or username", passes without a token, as the Understanding document allows. On real pages it found newsletter and sign-in fields without tokens on mozilla.org, kabum.com.br, saucedemo.com and wordpress.org, and passed GitHub's sign-in.

## 1.4.5 Images of Text

- **axe-core checks** nothing for 1.4.5: whether a picture is mostly text is a question about pixels.
- **Rampa judges** every rendered picture larger than an icon (more than 40 px on a side): `img`, image buttons, named `canvas`, elements with `role="img"` and no text of their own, and CSS background images (captured with the element's own content hidden, so only the picture is judged). Inline SVG is left out, since its text is real text.
- **Logos are left out:** logotypes are essential by definition, so a picture whose alternative, file name, id or class says it is a logo is not judged, nor one inside a link (itself or up to four levels up) to a site's home page ("/" or a language root such as "/pt-br/", on any site: a partner's logo links to its home too), nor, in a site check (`--crawl` or `--sitemap`), one the crawl had already seen on two other pages or more, by the same capture or the same address. Which pages count depends on what the crawl had read when the page was judged; the snapshot records it (`siteImages`).
- **OCR first** (`rampa/ocr`): each picture is read with [tesseract.js](https://github.com/naptha/tesseract.js) 7.0.0 and its English LSTM data (`@tesseract.js-data/eng` 1.0.0, the 4.0.0 "best int" model), both pinned optional dependencies. It runs locally: the data comes from the package, never from a CDN, and nothing is written to disk. Small pictures are enlarged up to four times, and two passes are read, the automatic layout and sparse text, which finds a label on a button. Words count with Tesseract's confidence at 60 or more and two letters or more; words that are real text the page draws over the picture (a carousel's caption, found by its box) are left out. Then the text share: of the picture's detail (pixels where a color channel steps by 48 or more to the next pixel: letter strokes, edges, the texture of a photo), the part inside the boxes of the words read, padded for outlines and shadows. A picture with no word of three letters or more, or whose words are less than half its detail, cannot be an image of text by Rampa's measure and passes without a model: a photo with a sign, a book cover, a product shot with a headline. Each capture is read once per run, by its hash.
- **Context for the model:** the picture as it renders, how it is drawn, its size, its alternative and its file name, and nothing else of the page, since its verdict is cached by the picture's hash (`Criterion.judgmentKey`) and the prompt's: a picture repeated on a page or across a crawl with the same alternative and file name is judged once, and its copies share one call. The same picture with another alternative is asked again, and so is every picture after a change to the prompt.
- **Verification:** the model must transcribe the text it reads. A claim needs at least three letters, since a lone character or a pair of initials is a symbol or a monogram, like the Understanding document's "B" for bold. It is dropped when a descriptive alternative (three content words or more, generic words such as "banner" left out) shares no word with the transcription, when the page itself calls the picture a screenshot, chart, diagram, graph or map, when the same words are shown as real text next to the picture (the Understanding document says that meets the criterion; ACT 0va7u6's proposed version calls it the "redundant" exception), and when they are real text drawn over it. Then OCR must agree: at least half of the transcribed words of three letters or more must be among the words OCR read, allowing a slip in every four letters, missing accents and a word cut short at the edge ("Roc" for "Rocks"), and the picture must be mostly text. When OCR could not run (tesseract.js not installed, a picture over 4 megapixels once enlarged), a claim is kept below the default confidence threshold.
- **Patch:** replaces an `img` with a `<span>` holding its text, or an image button with a `<button>`; the look comes back with CSS. Backgrounds and canvas get no patch.
- **Evaluation** (Gemma 4 12B, 2026-10-11, ACT `a9a1483e`): 0va7u6 is scored on its proposed version, whose "redundant" exception passes the test case that shows "Welcome" as an image above the paragraph "Welcome to our website" (Failed Example 4 in the published JSON, Passed Example 9 on the proposed rule page). Precision 1.00 and recall 0.75 (3 of 4), as before OCR (1.00 and 0.60 on the approved version's outcomes, for the same verdicts). The miss is a model error: the 80 × 20 px picture of "WCAG Rocks", which OCR reads, is called a logotype. OCR passed 5 of the 10 candidates without a model (photos, the gothic "Ivanhoe" cover, the "A" buttons); pairs that swap pictures for a rendered sentence (`text-as-image`): 4 of 4.
- **On real pages** (the real-page study's 12 dev pages, 1.4.5 alone, 2026-10-11; labels are the author's reading, not a person's review): before OCR, 125 candidates, 125 model calls and 4 findings; with OCR, 121 candidates, 96 passed by OCR, 25 model calls and 3 findings, the same pages' wall time 416 s before and 184 s after. The 3 are image ads in ad frames on drauziovarella.uol.com.br, two of them the same ad (an `img` and the background of the box around it). The four before were two of those ads, a promotional card on ufc.br that pairs a collage of photos with a headline, which OCR cannot read, and an npr.org app ad whose headline sits beside a phone screenshot, whose words OCR measures at 38% of its detail. OCR took 0.2 to 4 s a page (27 pictures on npr.org), and 61 pictures over a 5-page crawl of ufc.br sent 2 to the model, the site's repeated pictures left out from the third page on.
- **Why it is not on by default:** EVAL-1 in the coverage plan asks for 35 findings reviewed by a person with no false positive, and these runs give 3. OCR removed the cost argument (it doubled the image work of 1.1.1 before); precision on real pages is still unmeasured.
- **Limits:** OCR reads the Latin alphabet with English data; text in other scripts, small or stylized lettering on a busy background (a halftone ad on agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br, the ufc.br card) is not read, and such a picture is never reported. The text share is a measure of detail, not of meaning: a button's border and gradient count as detail that is not text ("Press me!" in ACT 0va7u6 measures 0.53), and a bold headline over a plain photo can measure as mostly text. A picture captured with something drawn over it that is not in its box in the snapshot (a donation box over 17 article photos on npr.org) is read as that, and the model is asked; a descriptive alternative that shares no word with the transcription still drops the claim. The transcription is the model's reading and can slip on fine print. Promotional banners whose text dominates are reported; teams that read them as pictures with significant other content can waive them. tesseract.js and its data add about 65 MB to an install (`npm install --omit=optional` leaves them out, and 1.4.5 then keeps every claim below the threshold).

## 2.4.2 Page Titled

- **axe-core checks** that the page has a non-empty title. It passes "Untitled document".
- **Rampa judges** a non-empty title against the page's address, first headings and opening text. In a site check (`rampa check --crawl` or `--sitemap`), the titles of up to ten other pages the crawl has read, the closest by address first, go along, and a title that matches one of them, or differs only by the site name, does not tell the page apart. Which pages are listed depends on what the crawl had read when the page was judged.
- **Titles of the pages it links to:** with or without a crawl, the titles Rampa read at the addresses of the page's links for 2.4.4 (`--follow-links`, same-origin by default) join that list, after the crawl's: pages of the same site that answered with HTML, at another address than the page's own (another query, a fragment, a trailing `index.html` or slash name the same page). A file, a page not found and a sign-in tell nothing. They are read without running scripts, so a page that sets its title by script shows the title its HTML gives. A home page leaves them out: its title is the site's name, which pages that carry only that name share with it (G88).
- **Home pages:** on a site's root ("/") or a language root ("/pt", "/pt-br/", "/en-US/index.html"), the name of the site or organization describes the page (technique G88, Understanding 2.4.2). When the title, or a part of it between separators such as " - " or " | ", is that name as the address spells it (the host, "GOV.BR" on www.gov.br; a label of it, "MIT" on www.mit.edu; or the initials of a name, "Universidade Federal do Ceará" on www.ufc.br), the title passes without a model; in the coverage this is a method of kind `rule`, `rampa/home-page-title`. A generic title such as "Home" still goes to the model, and so does a home page whose name the address does not spell, with a line in the prompt that it is a home page. These were the real-page study's two 2.4.2 false positives on its dev split.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the title; a fail needs a problem and a different suggested title under 140 characters.
- **Patch:** replaces the text of `<title>`.
- **Limits:** the syntax set 2779a5 titles its passing pages "Title of the page.", which Rampa rightly says describes nothing, so its precision there is a scoring artifact.

## 2.4.4 Link Purpose (In Context)

- **axe-core checks** that a link has a name. It passes "Click here".
- **Rampa judges** links with text (links whose only content is an image are left to 1.1.1) with their programmatically determined context: the paragraph, list items or cell around them, the heading above, the landmark, `aria-describedby` and the target of a same-page link. It also reads where each link leads (title, first heading, description, file type) and, for links that share a text, how many places they lead to and whether their context tells them apart; see [link-purpose.md](link-purpose.md).
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the link text; a fail needs a problem and a different suggested text; a mismatch needs a known destination, and an ambiguous claim needs the link-set fact behind it. A mismatch must rest on what was read, never on the address text alone: the part of this page a "#part" link points to, or a destination page with a title or main heading that tells it apart. A title that is the site's name (this page's own title, or a site root's), a title or heading read at several different addresses, and a not-found page (status 404 or 410, a redirect to a "/404" path, or a title such as "Página não encontrada") give no evidence, so the real-page study's "CULTURA" read as the university's name, three category tiles all read as "Categorias", and a link redirecting to /404 are no longer reported.
- **Findings and suggestions:** a verified fail is a finding only when a fact Rampa checks supports its problem:
  - `generic`: the text is generic and the link has no programmatically determined context of its own: no sentence, paragraph, list item or cell around it (as the prompt gives them), no header cells and no description, so nothing can complete the text. A generic text is one on the list ("click here", "read more", "saiba mais", "leia mais", "más información"...) or one made only of words that say "follow me": a verb that names no destination ("click", "see", "read", "check", "veja", "confira", "acesse") with a word that only points ("here", "this", "it", "more", "aqui", "mais"), such as "Check it out" or "Clique aqui para ver mais" (technique F84's non-specific link text). Whether a context completes a generic text is the model's reading, so such a claim is a suggestion: "Clique aqui" in "Clique aqui para mais informações sobre a Ouvidoria da EBC." was one of the dev pages' false positives.
  - `generic`, also: the text names only what kind of file it gets, never which document (a format such as "HTML", "EPUB", "Plain text" or "PDF", the act of getting it, "Download" or "Baixar", a size with its unit, and filler; a number without a unit, such as "2024 (PDF)", names a document), and nothing in its programmatically determined context could say which: no sentence, paragraph, list item or cell text, no description, no heading above it, it is not in the site's navigation, header or footer at any depth, nor an item of a menu (a list of links that do not all name a file's kind, as debian.org's "Download" among "About Debian" and "News" was), and either it names a format and has no header cells, or it sits in a table row whose header cells do not name what the rest of the row does, a data cell beside it with a name that no row header carries (technique F63: the context is in content that is not the link's programmatically determined context). A "Download" with no format is read only in such a row: elsewhere it names an action or a download page. ACT 5effbb's Failed Examples 5 ("HTML", "EPUB" and "Plain text" under a paragraph styled as the book's title) and 6 ("Download" in a row whose book title is a data cell, under the header "Books") are this fact. A specific word such as "Workshop" with its description in another paragraph (Failed Example 4) is not: whether a word names its destination is the model's reading, and on real pages that reading was wrong for "ISTs", "TOC" and "Download".
  - `url_or_filename`: the text is an address with a path or a query, or a file name. A bare host such as "www.example.org" or "ufc.br" names the site it leads to, so a claim about it is a suggestion.
  - `ambiguous`: other links with the same text lead elsewhere, and nothing in this link's context tells them apart (ACT fd3a94). The context compared is the link's sentence, paragraph, list item or cell, its header cells, its description, and the headings of the sections it sits in, so two "Play" links under two "Result" headings tell apart when each "Result" is in the section of its own example.
  - `mismatch` only for a link to a part of this page: the page names that part (a word of its id is a word of its text, the site's own name and words for how a page is built aside: `#hours` over "Opening hours: Monday to Friday"; a heading's part is the section it opens), and no word of the link's text, context, heading, header cells or description is one of the part's words or the id's, also in another form ("specs" and "Specifications"), nor a word in capitals the initials of the heading or the id spell ("FAQ" and "Frequently asked questions"). Left out, since they name a region or a control, not a subject: a part that is a landmark, a control or a form field, that sits in the site's navigation, header, footer, search or a form, or that holds one of these (a wrapper around the menu, a search box, a feedback form); a link whose text moves within the page or answers a widget ("Skip to…", "Ir para…", "Back to top", a carousel's "Próximo", "Sim"); and a generic text, an address or a number. A table of contents entry that points to another section fails this way, and so do the link-mismatch pairs. The fact reads words: a synonym or a translation of the part's name ("Contato" for "Fale conosco") satisfies it too, and then only the model's verdict decides. A mismatch with another page never is: a title or heading read at the address is a summary, no proof of what the text promises (a privacy notice that holds the cookie statement the link names, a page about the setting the link's sentence describes). On the real-page study's pages every such claim was wrong, and on the fresh-page study's dev pages the five specific link texts that share no word with the page they lead to were none of them a mismatch ("Report a problem with this page" to "Feedback", "Right of withdrawal" to "Terms and conditions", "Data for GDP" to the series "Gross Domestic Product").

  Any other fail is the model's suggestion: listed under "Needs review" with the model and the text it suggests, never a failure. See [notes-244-246.md](studies/notes-244-246.md) for the measurements behind this.
- **Patch:** replaces the link text, or `aria-label` when the name comes from it. A suggestion carries the suggested text, not a patch.
- **Evaluation:** on 2026-10-23 with Gemma 4 12B (one run, links followed on the W3C host), the findings caught 5 of 6 failed examples of 5effbb (recall 0.83, precision 1.00: the generic "More" and "Go", the formats and the "Download" of Failed Examples 5 and 6), 7 of 8 of fd3a94 and every one of c487ae (axe-core's), and told apart 20 of the 21 link pairs (11 made generic, 9 of the 10 pointed elsewhere). Scored without the new facts, the same answers give what the run of 2026-10-11 gave (3 of 6 failed examples of 5effbb, 11 pairs): the facts of the formats, the table row and the part of the page made the difference ([notes](studies/notes-remaining-errors.md)). A fresh run with the facts as a review of the change narrowed them, the demo's recorded evaluation (2026-10-23), gives the same numbers. "Workshop" (Failed Example 4) and the pair the model passed, "Go to the main content." on an SVG link, are not caught; the first is a suggestion. The 9 pairs pointed elsewhere count only because of a fact written after reading their corruption (below): they are no held-out measure.
- **Limits:** a broken link is never a finding, because it is not a WCAG failure. ACT's fd3a94 accepts copies of a page and links "ambiguous to everyone" that Rampa reports: its two false positives (precision 0.78, recall 0.88) are Passed Examples 5 and 9, links with one name that lead to two pages ACT reads as equivalent. The mismatch claims on Inapplicable Examples 3 and 5 ("Contact us" leading to a page titled "My University") and the claim on Passed Example 7 are suggestions, not findings. The link-mismatch pairs all point to one paragraph whose id and text name it (`#hours`, "Opening hours"), the shape the part-of-the-page fact checks, and the fact was written after reading that corruption: the pairs measure the fact as much as the model, and are no held-out measure. On the fresh-page study's dev pages that fact held for none of the 32 links to a part of the page (31 skip links and an audio player's link), but its guards were written after reading those links, so that is no held-out measure either: its precision on real pages is not measured. It reads words, so a synonym or a translation of the part's name satisfies it, and then only the model's verdict decides; a mis-pointed link with a numbered id (`#section-3`) never satisfies it.

## 2.4.6 Headings and Labels

- **axe-core checks** that headings are not empty and fields have a label. It passes "Section 2" over reviews and "Field 1" on an email field.
- **Rampa judges** every heading against the content it introduces (up to the next heading of its level or the end of its section), and every field label against the field.
- **Context of a heading:** its parent heading and the sections above that, its sibling headings, and the numbered series it belongs to: the headings of the page that differ from it only by a number, such as "Example 1" to "Example 7". A heading in a header, footer, navigation, aside or dialog takes its parent only from that region. The prompt says that a heading in a numbered series or among parallel headings ("Do" and "Don't") passes under a parent heading that names their subject, and that a heading naming the part a section plays ("Introduction") describes it.
- **Which label:** the text people see. When a field's name comes from `aria-label` and a visible `label` or a placeholder shows, the visible text is judged and patched; whether the two match is 2.5.3 (Label in Name), and a name nobody sees is 3.3.2. A field with only `aria-label` is judged on it.
- **Cut-off text:** the collector records how much of a heading, `label` or `legend` shows when its box cuts it off (an ellipsis, a line clamp, a fixed box with hidden overflow). The prompt then says what people see, such as "Returns and refund…", and asks the model to judge that too.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the heading or label text; a fail needs a problem and a different suggestion under 160 characters. A "says nothing" claim on a heading of a numbered series is dropped when its parent heading names a subject (it is not a placeholder, nor a number with at most one word, such as "Part 1"), so the real-page study's "Example 1" to "Example 7" are no longer reported.
- **Findings and suggestions:** a verified `generic` or `placeholder_text` fail is a finding only when Rampa itself can tell that the text names nothing: a placeholder or a numbering on the generic lists ("Lorem ipsum", "Section 2", "Title", "Field 1"); a heading that is a number with at most one word ("Part 3", "Example 4") in a numbered series under no parent heading that names their subject; or a label several fields share with no visible heading or group to tell them apart (ACT cc0f0a). A `mismatch` ("names a different topic than the content under it") is never a finding: a heading shares no word with its section on a third of the real headings of the study's dev pages ("Em alta", "Introduction", "Navegue por categoria"), so no fact separates a real mismatch from a good heading. Any other fail is the model's suggestion, listed under "Needs review", never a failure.
- **Patch:** replaces the heading or label text, the placeholder, or `aria-label`. A suggestion carries the suggested text, not a patch.
- **Evaluation:** on 2026-10-11 with Gemma 4 12B (one run), the findings caught 1 of 6 failed examples of cc0f0a (shared labels under headings moved off the screen), none of b49b2e's 4 and 5 of the 9 heading pairs (headings renamed "Part 1"), with no false positive. The model failed the other 4 of cc0f0a ("Menu", "Info:"), all 4 of b49b2e ("Weather") and 3 more pairs (labels renamed "Entry 1"), all as suggestions, plus one false suggestion ("A" over an alphabetical list).
- **Limits:** a heading or label that names the wrong thing (ACT b49b2e's "Weather" over opening hours, cc0f0a's "Menu" on a first-name field) is only a suggestion, so a run reports it for review, not as a failure.

## 2.5.3 Label in Name

Experimental, on request (`--criteria 2.5.3`): every failure is reported at low confidence, below the default threshold. The 2.5.3 rules run without it.

- **axe-core checks** controls named by their content (`label-content-name-mismatch`). Rampa's rules add a field whose tied label an `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` overrides (`rampa/field-label-in-name`) and an input button whose value an `aria-label` renames (`rampa/button-label-in-name`); [rules.md](rules.md) has both.
- **What none of them sees:** the label people read when it is plain text next to the field, not tied to it: "Email" in a `span` before an input that `aria-label` names "Your address", or that only a placeholder names. A person using speech input says "Email", and nothing answers.
- **Candidates:** a field (text, select, checkbox, radio...) on screen, with a name and no visible label tied to it (a `label` for it or around it, or `aria-labelledby` to visible text), whose closest visible text in reading order, with no other field, control or heading between, sits right beside it as the boxes measure it: on its left on the same line, or above it and aligned with it, within 48 px (24 px above); on its right for a checkbox or a radio. The text is short (six words, 60 characters), is not a sentence (four words or more ending in a stop), belongs to no control or heading, labels no other field, and is not in the field's name: its words in a row, `*` and "(required)" aside, and not a near match (a hyphen, a shortened word), which the rules send to review.
- **The question:** whether the text is the field's label, the words a person would use to call it, or something else: an instruction, a unit, a question over several fields, the label of another field.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the text found; a fail must say the text labels the field, and the name must not contain it.
- **Message and patch:** quotes the text, the name and where the name comes from. The patch sets `aria-label` to the text on an input, so the name holds what people see; a name from `aria-labelledby` gets no patch, since it would win over the attribute.
- **Evaluation** (Gemma 4 12B, 2026-10-11): on 2ee8b8, axe-core's own set, the judgment finds no candidate and adds nothing: precision 0.89 and recall 1.00 (n = 38), axe-core's scores. Pairs (`label-detached`) take cc0f0a's passing pages and turn each label into a `span` in the same place, with the field renamed in `aria-label` ("Entry 1"): 4 of 4, against 0 for axe-core (7 fields, all failed; the intact pages give no candidate).
- **On real pages:** no candidate on rampa.guilhermebs.com.br, www.gov.br, www.gov.uk/register-to-vote, www.sosma.org.br/contato (its fields show only placeholders), www.accessnow.org/contact-us, www.usa.gov and www.nhs.uk. On docs.python.org the language `select`, named "Language", sat right of the breadcrumb's "Python" and was a candidate until a text and a field in two items of one list were left out.
- **Limits:** a label far from its field, below it, in another item of a list, or with other text between them is not seen; nor is a label drawn as an image. When the closest text is a mark such as an asterisk in its own element, the label before it is not reached. Placeholder-only labels are left to the rules' review.

## 3.1.1 Language of Page

- **axe-core checks** that the page declares a valid language. It passes `lang="en"` on a page in Portuguese.
- **Rampa judges** the text that inherits the root language against the declared language. That text follows ACT ucwvc8: the page title, the text in reading order, and the names and descriptions set in attributes (`alt`, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, `title`, `aria-describedby`); text under an element with its own non-empty `lang` is left out, and an empty `lang` declares nothing.
- **Language identifier:** an n-gram identifier (ELD, small database, 60 languages) reads the same text first. It was chosen over franc by measuring both on 1,246 texts in pt, en, es, nl, fr and de: ELD read 87% of the texts under 16 characters and 97% of those up to 40 correctly, franc-min 23% and 69%. A long text it reads clearly (150 letters, 20 words, one language well ahead and no large part in another) is decided without a model: a mismatch fails, and the message says so. Short or mixed text goes to the model. Languages it has no n-grams for, and pairs it confuses (Danish, Norwegian and Swedish; Croatian, Serbian and Bosnian; Malay and Indonesian; Czech and Slovak), are never decided by it. In the per-criterion coverage, what it decided is a method of kind `rule`, `rampa/language-id`, apart from the judgment: with judgment on and no model configured, 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 are then checked by that rule instead of "not checked". With `--no-llm` it decides nothing.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be an excerpt of the title or the text, of three letters at least unless it is all of one; the detected language must be a valid BCP 47 tag, different from the declared one on a fail and the same on a pass. A model's fail is dropped when a confident reading (60 letters or more) says the text is in the declared language or in another language than the model named, and when the declared language fits the words as well as any other ("Paul put dire comment on tape" is English and French, so the page has no default language).
- **Patch:** sets the root `lang` to the detected primary subtag.
- **Other language versions:** with `--probe variants`, the versions of the page in other languages that it links to (`hreflang` alternates, a language switcher) are loaded and read by the same identifier, clear readings only (rule `rampa/language-versions`, [probes](probes.md#state-variants-111-and-311---probe-variants-rules-rampacarousel-name-and-rampalanguage-versions)).
- **Evaluation:** ucwvc8 precision 0.56 and recall 1.00 (0.50 and 1.00 before the identifier); the four false positives left are axe-core failures on inapplicable cases, which the scoring fix in the coverage plan (A2) removes. Pairs (`html-lang-swap`): 4 of 4.

## 3.1.2 Language of Parts

- **axe-core checks** that `lang` values are valid. It passes a Dutch review marked `lang="es"`.
- **Rampa judges** each element with a valid `lang` whose text may be in another language, allowing the criterion's exceptions (proper names, technical terms, words of indeterminate language, vernacular). A long element the language identifier reads clearly is decided without a model, as for 3.1.1.
- **Unmarked passages:** text with no `lang` of its own that inherits the page's language is read by the identifier in windows of five words, so a quote inside a sentence is found as well as a whole paragraph. Runs it reads as another language (20 letters and 3 words at least) go to the model with the text around them, and the model names the exception that covers them, if any; with no exception and another language, the passage fails. A passage under 60 letters stays below the confidence threshold. At most eight per page, the longest first.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be an excerpt of the element's text, of three letters at least unless it is all of it; a fail needs a different, valid detected language and no exception. The identifier drops a model's fail as for 3.1.1; a pass that names an exception is left to the model.
- **Patch:** sets the element's `lang`; for a passage, the block's `lang`, or a `<span lang>` around a phrase.
- **Evaluation:** off6ek precision 1.00 and recall 1.00 (0.80 and 1.00 before: "Paul put dire comment on tape" marked French is no longer failed). Pairs: `lang-swap` 5 of 5 (4 of 5 before); `lang-drop`, which takes the `lang` off every passage in another language than the page, 1 of 5: two of its passages are that two-language sentence, which rightly passes, one is "Bonne année !", too short to nominate, and a Dutch phrase between two English spans was not failed.
- **Limits:** a phrase of one or two words in another language is not nominated; only the model, on a marked element, can judge it.

## 3.3.1 Error Identification

- **What it reads:** error messages a page already shows: what a field in an error state names with `aria-errormessage` or `aria-describedby`, text with an error class or `role="alert"` near fields, and text in a form worded as an error ("Invalid value for age.", "Please fill the field correctly.", "CPF inválido"), at most 10 a page. Hidden text is not a message: on a page as it loads, a hidden message is a template waiting for an error. Nothing is submitted ([rules](rules.md#error-states-331-and-333) has what counts as an error state).
- **Rampa asks** whether the text is an error message at all (an instruction written before anything is entered is not), and, per ACT 36b590, whether a person can tell which field is in error and whether the message says what is wrong or how to fix it. "Invalid value", "Error" and "Please fill the field correctly" say there is an error, not what it is.
- **Context:** the message; whether it is hidden from assistive technology; the fields of its form with their labels, types, groups, `required` and `aria-invalid`; which fields it is tied to (by their own attributes or by their fieldset's), and the fields just before and after it.
- **Settled without the model:** a message the model takes for an error that is hidden from assistive technology (`aria-hidden`) fails ACT 36b590's third expectation, whatever else the model says.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the message; a quoted cause must be words of it, and a named field one of the form's labels or group names. A claim that the message names no field is dropped when the message is tied to one field (or one group), or when one of its words names exactly one field ("Name and color cannot be empty" with one Name field and a color group). A claim that it says nothing about the cause is dropped when it has words that do ("cannot be empty", "must", "at least", "format", a number).
- **Experimental:** findings are capped at low confidence, below the default threshold, until the module is measured on real pages, and the module is not a default.
- **Evaluation:** ACT 36b590 (proposed), 9 test cases, Gemma 4 12B: precision 1.00, recall 0.80, in one run and again with three runs a candidate and an empty cache (18 calls). Failed Examples 1, 2, 4 and 5 are found; Failed Example 3's message is hidden with `display: none`, which on a page as it loads Rampa reads as a template, not an error, and leaves alone by design. Passed Example 2 ("Name and color cannot be empty. Please complete all required fields.") was failed by the model as naming no field, and that claim was dropped by verification. On two public pages that show errors on load (rules.md has them), the GOV.UK date message, which the model called unidentified, was dropped as tied to its fieldset; on Bootstrap's validation page the six server-side messages ("Please choose a username.", "Please provide a valid city."…) passed, and four messages of its "Supported elements" demo ("Example invalid feedback text", "Example invalid select feedback"…) failed as saying nothing about the error: true of their text, though they are placeholders in documentation.
- **Limits:** messages a page shows only after submission, or only in the browser's own validation bubble, are not seen; the COGA wording advisory the cognitive profile plan pairs with this work is not built.

## 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions

- **axe-core checks** that a field has an accessible name (rules `label` and `select-name`, under 4.1.2) and no more than one label (`form-field-multiple-labels`). A name in `aria-label` passes and shows nothing on screen, which the Understanding document calls a 3.3.2 failure: labels must be presented to everyone, not only to screen reader users.
- **Rampa judges** two kinds of field. A field whose name nobody sees: it comes from `aria-label`, `title` or a label hidden from view, and the field has no placeholder and, for a select, no option on show. And a field with a `pattern`: whether something visible states the format or shows an example of it. Spam honeypots are left out, as for 1.3.5: a visible label would show the trap to people.
- **Context:** where the name comes from, the placeholder or the option a select shows, the pattern, the visible text just before and after the field (labels of other fields left out), the heading above, the fieldset legend, and the buttons beside it, with whether they show text or only an icon.
- **Labels measured by their pixels:** a label is hidden from view when it is not rendered, sits off the page or is squeezed to a pixel, and also when it is on screen but shows nothing. The collector captures each label 3.3.2 relies on (a `label` tied to a field it judges, or an element a field's `aria-labelledby` names) as rendered, and again with its text transparent, `::before` and `::after` included and a wrapped field's own text kept as it was. When no more than three pixels change by more than 10 in a color channel, the text shows nothing: white on white, a visually-hidden class that clips with `clip-path` and keeps the label's size, a label inside a box with no height, `text-indent` out of the box, or something painted over it (fixed layers are hidden while it is captured, as for images). The snapshot records it on the label as `native.textVisible`, and 3.3.2 and the rule `rampa/no-visible-label` read a label whose text does not show as hidden, with a message that says how Rampa knows. Up to 30 labels a page, two screenshots each, with no model; it runs in `rampa check` and in `checkPage` alike, and a saved snapshot replays it.
- **Two more questions, experimental** (their failures stay below the default threshold until measured on real pages):
  - **An option group with no visible question:** radios, or checkboxes, that share a name in one form, two or more on screen, each with a visible label, with no legend and no group name, and nothing visible right before the first option (no text and no heading between it and the field, button or start of form before it). The model reads the options' labels and the heading above, and says whether they make the choice clear by themselves ("Credit card", "Bank slip"; "I agree to the terms") or not ("Yes", "No"). A fail must quote no visible text that says what the options choose. No patch: the fix is a `fieldset` with a `legend` around the options.
  - **A date typed with no format shown:** a text field (not `type="date"`, no `pattern`, no date picker class) whose label, name, id or `autocomplete` token (`bday`, `cc-exp`) says date, birth or expiry, in English, or in Portuguese or Spanish for a field in those languages ("data" is a date only in Portuguese), when nothing visible shows a format: the label, the placeholder, the text before and after the field and its description name no part of a date ("DD", "MM/AA", "dia", "year") and show no example ("01/02/2000"). The model says whether the field asks for a whole date, and in what format; the patch adds that format as a hint tied with `aria-describedby`.
- **Verification:** the evidence must be the field's name. A missing-label claim is dropped when the snapshot shows a visible label for the field, when the field's name is shown on screen right beside it (a visible label not tied to the field still labels it; the tie is 1.3.1), and when the model quotes visible text that names the field. A pattern claim needs a pattern, a statement of what it requires, and no visible word that the pattern accepts, since an example in the required format explains it.
- **Patch:** adds a visible `<label>` before an input, or a hint tied with `aria-describedby`. A `select` or `textarea` gets no patch, since its start tag is not the whole element.
- **Evaluation:** no ACT rule covers 3.3.2. Pairs take cc0f0a's passing pages, where every field has a visible label, and move each label into `aria-label` (`label-hidden`): 7 of 7, against 0 for axe-core. A second corruption keeps every label where it is and paints its text in the color behind it (`label-unseen`): 7 of 7 with the pixel test, 0 of 7 without it, and 0 for axe-core. The intact pages give the module no candidates, so they test the corruption, not false positives; the example pages in `examples/labels-or-instructions/` cover the pattern rule and an icon search button. With the option groups and dates added (Gemma 4 12B, 2026-10-11), the pairs give the same, 7 of 7 and 7 of 7, and the prompts of the fields judged before are unchanged. No cc0f0a page has an option group or a date typed as text, so those two questions are measured only by `test/fixtures/criteria/structure.html`; on www.gov.br, www.gov.uk/register-to-vote, www.sosma.org.br/contato, www.usa.gov, www.accessnow.org/contact-us and rampa.guilhermebs.com.br they found no candidate.
- **Limits:** a field labeled only by its placeholder is not reported: the WAI forms tutorial advises against it, but the Understanding document does not call it a failure. Icons drawn in CSS pseudo-elements are not in the snapshot, so the model cannot see them. The pixel test leaves out labels that hold a picture (an icon can be the label people see, and transparent text would not change it), labels past the thirtieth of a page, and the text of fields themselves; text drawn in an `svg` inside a label is not made transparent. A label that only shows at low contrast still counts as shown: that is 1.4.3's to measure. On 13 public pages it found no false positives after a select showing "English" beside a globe icon was taken out of scope, and few candidates at all, since most fields there have visible labels or placeholders.

## Questions a probe leaves to a model

Some probe rules ([probes](probes.md)) find a likely failure from what they measured, while one part of the criterion turns on meaning: an exception, a warning, whether a text is a status message. For eight criteria a module asks a model that one question. These modules are not chosen with `--criteria`: `rampa check` adds one when the snapshot holds its probe's record with something to ask, and only with judgment on (never with `--no-llm`). With no `--probe`, none of them runs. Their answers go through the same JSON schema, verification and cache as the other modules, and what they decide stays experimental, below the default threshold, like the probe rules themselves.

| Criterion | Probe | What the model is asked | What its answer can do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1.1 Keyboard | `parity` | The keys a control's pattern uses did not do what a click did. Whether what the keys did reaches the same function in other words, or whether the function needs the path of the pointer (a signature pad), which the criterion exempts | A verified pass clears the rule's finding: an "equivalent" pass must name a key path that did something and cite facts of both sides, a "path-dependent" pass must quote a listed text. A fail keeps it |
| 2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts | `shortcuts` (in `--probe all`) | Printable keys change the page, and the probe saw a shortcut setting it could not reach, or a control named for settings. Whether a control on show clearly leads to a way to turn the shortcuts off or remap them (ACT ffbc54) | A pass clears the rule's finding; a fail keeps it, with the model's answer in the evidence |
| 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable | `motion` | The page's time ran and the page ended what the person was doing, with no warning or no way to extend. Whether the page's own words about time show an exception (a real-time event, an essential limit, a limit over 20 hours) or a way to turn the limit off or adjust it | A pass must quote that text, and clears the finding; a fail keeps it |
| 2.4.3 Focus Order | `focus-order` | The Tab sequence holds a positive `tabindex` that reorders it (F44), or a group whose stops Tab visits going back up or left at least twice. Whether the order preserves meaning and operation | A fail must cite transitions, each recomputed from the recorded stops; it becomes a finding built from those transitions, not from the model's words, capped at low confidence |
| 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures | `gestures` | A swipe or pinch changed an element, on two loads, and no control around it does the same with a single pointer. Whether the gesture is essential: a signature, freehand drawing, a game whose point is the gesture | A pass must quote a listed text, and clears the finding; a fail keeps it |
| 3.2.2 On Input | `input` | Changing a control's value changed the context, on two loads. Whether a text around the control or on the page tells the person, before they use it, what the change will do (G13) | A pass must quote that text, and clears the finding; a fail keeps it |
| 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum) | `auth` | A sign-in or recovery step shows text that may ask a cognitive function test: some characters of a secret (F109), a calculation, the transcription of an image. Whether the step does ask such a test, or the text is help, an example, or a test the criterion exempts (recognizing objects, or content the person provided) | Either answer must quote the page. A fail keeps the rule's finding with the model's quote; a pass clears it |
| 4.1.3 Status Messages | `status` | Text appeared after a safe trigger, and nothing exposes it: no live region, no status, alert or log role, no focus, no dialog. Whether it gives information on the result of an action, a wait, the progress of a process or an error | A verified answer settles the rule's item to review: a fail must quote the words that make it a status message and becomes a finding, with `role="status"` as the patch when the element was on the page at load; a pass removes the item, with a note |

Six of the eight can only clear a probe rule's finding, never make one. 4.1.3's and 2.4.3's can make a finding, and both stay below the default threshold. What each probe does, its rules and how they were measured are in [probes](probes.md), under the probe.
