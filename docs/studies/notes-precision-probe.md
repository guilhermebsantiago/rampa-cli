# Precision probe: where the false positives are, and what each idea would do (2026-10-23)

The three real-page studies ([first](real-pages-2026-10.md), [its re-measure](real-pages-2026-10-remeasure.md), [second](real-pages-2026-10-fresh.md), [third](real-pages-2026-10-round3.md)) leave the judgment layer's precision on real pages at 0.43 on the third study's dev pages and 0.24 on its test pages. These notes put every labelled finding of the three studies in one table, find the few causes that hold most of the false positives, re-run today's `main` (15b1149) on the third study's recorded snapshots, and simulate each candidate intervention on the labelled findings: deterministic facts, which claims should be findings at all, a second pass with a token probability, and the known patterns. Each is reported as precision and true positives kept, with 95% Wilson intervals.

**Every number here is in-sample.** All 72 pages of the three studies have now been read, including both test halves, so nothing below is a held-out measurement. Where an intervention was written from one study's patterns, its column for the other study is the closest thing to an out-of-sample check, and the notes say so each time. The labels are the studies' two Claude judges, not people. A pre-registered fourth round and a small blind human sample are what would confirm any of this (section 6).

**In short.**

- **Three causes hold 84% of the false positives a user sees today** (studies 2 and 3, the code since the 2.4.4/2.4.6 change: 137 false positives among 218 labelled findings): one page captured without its stylesheet, whose hidden type-ahead list became 50 "ambiguous link" findings (36%); the 1.1.1 claim "describes something the image does not show" on ordinary images, right 7 times in 59 (38%); and the functional-image claim "is all its link holds, and does not say where the link leads", right 18 times in 32 (10%). Across all three studies, `wrong_content` alone is a third of the 330 default-threshold false positives.
- **ufpe.br's block is one cause, and today's `main` does not remove it.** The theme CSS did not apply when the page was captured (the screenshot is unstyled HTML, 6,168 × 69,496 px); `ul#search-list`, a type-ahead suggestion list that the live page hides with `display: none`, was rendered as 1,628 visible links, 59,454 px tall, and the shared-text fact grounded 50 findings on it. The page loads with all ten stylesheets applied today, so the capture was transient and the snapshot has no record of why. Today's `main`, replayed on the same snapshot, reports the same 55 2.4.4 findings on the test half.
- **Today's `main` on the third study's snapshots**, with the recorded answers read back and 1.1.1 asked again under its version-5 prompt: dev 0.44 (15 TP / 19 FP), test 0.24 (24 / 77), against the study's 0.43 and 0.24. Thirteen 1.1.1 false positives left the default threshold (7 dropped, 6 moved below it), one true positive was lost, three new unlabelled 1.1.1 findings appeared. No measurable change, as the third study found for the code it measured.
- **What the simulations say** (pooled, 218 findings, precision 0.34 today): a capture guard for the unstyled page alone gives 0.45; with today's verification, the small facts and the 2.4.6/3.3.2/2.4.2 claims moved to suggestions, 0.65 (0.55–0.73) at a cost of 2 of 72 true positives; adding a one-word second pass on the 1.1.1 claims that rest on the model's reading, kept only when all three framings say fail, **0.83 (0.73–0.90) at a cost of 4 of 72 true positives** (third study: dev 0.83, test 0.77; second study 0.88). The purely deterministic alternative, sending every `wrong_content` claim to review, gives 0.81 at a cost of 7 true positives and of the ACT qt1vmo failed examples.
- **The confidence signal exists, but not where expected.** Ollama 0.40.3 returns a logprob for the first generated token only, so the verdict token of a JSON answer carries none; a second call whose whole answer is one word ("pass"/"fail", "yes"/"no") puts the verdict first, and its first-token distribution gives P(fail). On 101 decided 1.1.1 findings that probability separates true from false positives with AUROC 0.89–0.91 under three framings. On 2.4.4 it is useless (AUROC 0.47–0.60): the second pass agrees with the first on the hidden list, because nothing it sees says the list is hidden.
- **kcl.ac.uk crashed because of a bug in the undici that Node bundles**, not Rampa's code and not Playwright's: undici issue [#5360](https://github.com/nodejs/undici/issues/5360), `assert(!this.paused)` in the HTTP/1 parser's `finish()` when the server ends the connection while the body stream is paused under backpressure. Rampa reaches it through the global `fetch` that reads link destinations for 2.4.4. One live load today, in debug mode, crashed with the full stack inside `node:internal/deps/undici`. The fix is in undici 8.4.1 and `main` (PRs #5389 and #5474) and not in the 7.x line that Node 24 bundles (7.29.1 in 24.21.0, 7.30.1 on the v24.x branch).

## 4. The confidence signal

**Verbalized confidence** was "high" on every finding of the first study and on every 2.4.4 finding of the third; it carries nothing, as the deep-research report expects of a 12B model.

**Token probability.** Ollama 0.40.3 returns `logprobs` on `/api/generate`, `/api/chat` and `/v1/chat/completions`, but for the first generated token only, streamed or not, with or without a JSON schema (`format`), with Gemma 4 12B. Under `format` that first token is the grammar's `{`, whose own distribution is meaningless (the model's top token was a code fence at −0.0). So the verdict inside Rampa's JSON answer has no probability. The way round it is a second call whose whole answer is one word: the first token is then "pass" or "fail" (or "yes"/"no"), its `top_logprobs` hold the alternatives, and P(fail) is the mass on the fail words over the mass on both. `verifier.ts` did this for every labelled 1.1.1, 2.4.4 and 2.4.6 judgment finding of studies 2 and 3 (279 findings, 837 calls, no error, every first token an answer word; median 1.7–2.0 s a call, p90 2.9–8.5 s on a shared GPU), with three framings, each with the criterion's own page content and image:

- **A**, the criterion's decision rules without its output-format instructions, "answer with exactly one word, pass or fail";
- **B**, the finding as Rampa reported it, shown as a reviewer's claim, "is this claim correct? yes or no";
- **C**, the question asked the other way round ("does the current text alternative serve the same purpose as the image?", "can a person tell where this link leads?"), a fail being "no".

| Criterion (decided findings) | Framing | AUROC of P(fail) against the label | P(fail) ≥ 0.5: TP kept · FP kept | mean P(fail) on TP · on FP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1.1 (40 TP, 61 FP) | A | 0.89 | 37/40 · 33/61 | 0.93 · 0.57 |
| | B | 0.91 | 35/40 · 22/61 | 0.89 · 0.37 |
| | C | 0.89 | 36/40 · 27/61 | 0.90 · 0.45 |
| 2.4.4 (7 TP, 57 FP) | A | 0.60 | 7/7 · 57/57 | 1.00 · 0.99 |
| | B | 0.47 | 7/7 · 55/57 | 1.00 · 0.97 |
| | C | 0.54 | 7/7 · 54/57 | 1.00 · 0.95 |

On 1.1.1 the three framings agree on 34 of the 40 true positives and on only 12 of the 61 false positives (all three say fail). On 2.4.4 every framing calls the hidden ufpe.br list a failure, as the first pass did: a second opinion from the same model, on the same input, cannot see what the capture hid, which is the general limit of this signal. Agreement across framings and the mean or minimum probability rank the same findings; the three-votes rule and a 0.9 minimum give the same 123 / 4 in S8.

What this is not: a calibrated probability (the fail words sit near 1.0 for most true positives and for the hidden list), a held-out number (the framings were written and run once, but the data were known), or a measurement of what a different model would do. What it costs: three short calls per finding, not per candidate, so on the third study's 24 pages about 430 calls, a few minutes a page beside the 22–117 judgment calls each page already makes; one framing (B) alone gives most of the separation (AUROC 0.91) at a third of the cost, with 5 of 40 true positives lost at 0.5.

- **Interventions written from one study, measured on the other.** The 1.1.1 facts of the second study's dev half (T0) take 21 FP and 2 TP off the second study and 2 FP and 0 TP off the third: their in-sample 0.27 → 0.70 became 0.26 → 0.26 held out, which the third study already reported. The facts written here from the third study's pages (I3, I4, I5, I6) take, on the second study, 0 / 0 / 0 / 2 FP and 0 / 2 / 0 / 0 TP: I4 costs the second study two true positives that today's `main` loses anyway, the others barely fire. The capture guard (I1) fires on no second-study page. The second pass, whose prompts were written once, moves the second study by −37 FP / −3 TP and the third by −86 FP / −1 TP, and each half of the third in the same direction. That consistency is the evidence for it; it is not a held-out measurement.
- **A claim policy fitted on two studies and applied to the third** (`policy.py`, `data/policy.md`): keeping only the claims whose precision on studies 1 and 2 is at least 0.3 (the functional image, generic alternatives, 1.3.5, `ambiguous`) moves the third study from 0.29 to 0.34 and keeps 31 of 40 true positives; the same rule fitted on studies 1 and 3 moves the second from 0.44 to 0.63 and keeps 17 of 32; at 0.7 the second study reads 0.86 with 12 of 32 kept. Claim-level policies transfer when the failure mode is the claim (`wrong_content`) and not when it is one page (ufpe.br). Fitting on studies 2 and 3 and checking on study 1 gives 0.17 → 0.16: the first study's code reported claims that no longer exist as findings, so that direction says little.
- **Clustering.** The 50 ufpe.br findings are one event, as the first study's python.org.br 33 were. The Wilson intervals above treat findings as independent and are therefore too narrow; the deep-research report's design-effect arithmetic (five findings a page, ρ = 0.3) would widen them by about a third.

**A round 4, pre-registered**, as the third study was: 24 fresh pages from registrable domains no file of the repository names, the same strata and seeded split, the code frozen before collection, with the outcomes named in advance: precision at the default threshold, dev and test apart, for the stack that ships, and, for the second pass, the precision-against-coverage curve at the thresholds 0.5 and 0.9 fixed now. Two additions this probe suggests: record and report the capture's state (stylesheets that did not apply, an overlay over the viewport when pixels are measured, a load that timed out), since two of the three largest clusters of the third study's test half were capture events; and label a sample of the suggestions too, so the cost in recall of what moved to review is measured and not only its benefit in precision.

**A human-labelled sample**, as the deep-research report proposes: about 100 findings drawn at random, stratified by criterion and claim with no more than two findings from any one page, labelled blind by two people (the judges' labels and Rampa's patch and confidence hidden until both have decided), with a short codebook per criterion from the Understanding documents and a 20-finding calibration round discarded first; Cohen's kappa and raw agreement reported alongside Gwet's AC1, chosen in advance; disagreements adjudicated to a gold label with both raw labels kept. With precision near 0.4, 100 labels give a Wilson half-width of about 0.09 overall and about 0.17 per criterion for the criteria with 30 or more findings. That sample answers the question the three studies cannot: in which direction the two Claude judges err. If the auditor inherited the over-detection the report describes for LLM annotators, the true precision is lower than 0.34 and the interventions above are worth more; if the skeptic was too strict, some of the 137 "false positives" are real, and the ones to look at first are the six `wrong_content` claims every pass agrees on.

## 7. The kcl.ac.uk crash

**What happened.** During the third study, three runs on https://www.kcl.ac.uk/study/undergraduate exited with `rampa: The expression evaluated to a falsy value: assert(!this.paused)` (the main collection's first attempt at 11:07, and both probe attempts at 19:33), and two more recorded the site's "Rate limit exceeded. Please wait 10 minutes before retrying." page. The study set the page aside from the probe run.

**Where the assertion is.** `assert(!this.paused)` is in the HTTP/1 parser of undici, the HTTP client Node bundles behind the global `fetch` (`node:internal/deps/undici/undici`, three places: `execute()`, `finish()` and `onUpgrade()`). Rampa calls that `fetch` in `src/surfaces/destinations.ts` to read where links lead for 2.4.4: up to 40 addresses a page, four at a time, each response's body read to 512 KB then cancelled, a non-2xx body cancelled at once. One live load today in debug mode (`RAMPA_DEBUG=1`, the page loaded once, no probes) crashed again, with the stack:

```
AssertionError [ERR_ASSERTION]: The expression evaluated to a falsy value: assert(!this.paused)
    at Parser.finish (node:internal/deps/undici/undici:7388:9)
    at TLSSocket.onHttpSocketEnd (node:internal/deps/undici/undici:7827:34)
    at TLSSocket.emit (node:events:526:24)
    at endReadableNT (node:internal/streams/readable:1764:12)
```

**The cause.** undici issue [#5360](https://github.com/nodejs/undici/issues/5360) (2026-06-05): when the consumer has not yet read a response body, the parser pauses under backpressure; if the server then ends the connection (a response with `Connection: close`, or a close after the body), `onHttpSocketEnd` calls `parser.finish()`, which asserts the parser is not paused, from inside the socket's `end` handler, where nothing can catch it. The reporter notes the trigger is timing-dependent ("paused with the read buffer drained when FIN arrives"); kcl.ac.uk's server, or its CDN, closes connections that way, and the rate limiter's 429 page did too. It was fixed in undici 8.4.1 by [#5389](https://github.com/nodejs/undici/pull/5389) (2026-06-08) and reworked on `main` by [#5474](https://github.com/nodejs/undici/pull/5474) (2026-07-01), which makes `finish()` resume a paused parser and drain the socket. The 7.x line is not fixed: `lib/dispatcher/client-h1.js` on the `v7.x` branch (7.30.1) still asserts, and so does the copy in Node 24.21.0 (undici 7.29.1), which the study and this worktree run. A comment on the issue reports the same crash from Node 24.17 and 24.18.

**Whose bug.** Node's, through the undici it bundles; not Rampa's code, and not Playwright's (the browser never saw it: the crash is in the Node-side reader). Rampa's part is to be exposed: its reader uses the global `fetch` against sites it does not control, and 40 requests four at a time is also what made kcl.ac.uk rate-limit the study's repeated loads.

**Reproduction.** `kcl-repro-5360.mjs` and `kcl-repro-sizes.mjs` play the issue's own scenario and 16 variants (64 KiB to 4 MiB bodies, in one or many writes, `Connection: close`, FIN or RST, the body left unread, cancelled, or read then cancelled) against a local server with Node 24.21.0's `fetch`: none crashed, which fits the reporter's note that the trigger depends on when the FIN arrives relative to the parser's pause. The one live load above reproduced it; no further loads were made.

**What to do.** Read destinations with a client that has the fix: the `undici` package at 8.4.1 or later, with its own `fetch` (the test harness already injects a `fetch`, so the change is one import in `destinations.ts`), or Playwright's `context.request`, which the media probe already uses and which does not go through undici. Either way the reader should stay polite to a site: the per-origin pause on 429 and 503 exists, and a small delay between requests to one origin, or a lower concurrency per origin, would have spared kcl.ac.uk's limit. A process-level `uncaughtException` handler would turn the crash into a report with a note, but would not fix it, since the failed read's state is lost.

## 8. Frozen rules (2026-10-24), written before any new measurement

What was implemented on `probe/precision` after section 6, and the rules it fixes, so that round 4 measures them as they are:

- **The second pass** (`src/core/confirm.ts`, 1.1.1 only). It runs on a verified fail whose problem is `wrong_content` on an ordinary image, or `wrong_content` or `missing_information` on a functional image (an image that is all its link or button holds), never on a placeholder, a generic word, a `decorative` claim or the hidden-image question. Three framings, each a one-word question with the same picture and the same page content as the first pass: **rules** (the first pass's decision rules without its output format, "pass or fail"), **claim** (the finding as reported, "is the reviewer's claim correct? yes or no"), **reversed** ("does the alternative serve the image? yes or no", where "no" confirms). The prompts are the ones `verifier.ts` measured in section 4, word for word. **A finding when every framing answers with a confirming word and, where the provider reports token probabilities, the lowest probability of a confirming word over the three framings is at least 0.9.** On the data of section 4 the two conditions pick the same findings (S8 = S9); 0.99 would drop 3 more false positives and no true positive on 55 findings, and is not used. Ollama answers through its native API (`think: false`, two tokens at most, `top_logprobs: 10`); the probability is the mass on the confirming words over the mass on all allowed words at the first token. A provider that answers in text only decides on the words; a provider that cannot answer one-word questions (an agent CLI), an offline run with no cached answer, or a call that fails leaves the claim a suggestion. `--no-confirm` is the ablation.
- **Claims that are suggestions, not findings:** a 2.4.6 label several fields share; a 3.3.2 missing-label claim on a field that was off screen, beyond the viewport, in a box with no width or height, or a search field (a searchbox, `type="search"`, inside a search landmark, or named for searching); a 2.4.4 raw address with a sentence, list item, cell or description of its own.
- **Three 1.1.1 facts** that drop a `wrong_content` or `missing_information` claim before the second pass: a linked logo that leads to the site's root or a language root on the same site, saying "logo" or mostly the site's name; a flag named by a language code or name inside a language link (hreflang, a translation widget, or a language name in the link's text or title); for an image that is not a control's only content, an alternative every content word of which is in the text next to it.
- **The capture guards.** A page whose linked stylesheets did not apply (none applied, or none of the page's own) is loaded once more; if still unstyled, `reach.stylesheets.unstyled` is set, every judgment fail is a suggestion, and nothing is measured from pixels. An element on top at seven or more of nine viewport points, out of the flow or hosting a shadow root, holding at most half the page's text and no main landmark, is recorded as `reach.overlay` and hidden during captures.
- **Destinations** are read through undici 8, two per origin at a time, 250 ms apart.

What round 4 measures, pre-registered: precision at the default threshold per split and per criterion with the stack on, and with `--no-confirm` on the same snapshots (the ablation); the share of 1.1.1 reading claims the second pass confirms, denies or cannot ask; the capture state of every page (`reach.stylesheets`, `reach.overlay`); and a labelled sample of the suggestions, so what moved to review is costed in recall.
