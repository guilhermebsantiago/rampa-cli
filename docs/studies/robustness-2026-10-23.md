# Robustness study: 53 real sites, 2026-10-23

Rampa was run on the home page (or one public page) of 53 well-known sites, in three modes, to find crashes, hangs, time-outs, collector failures and wrong behaviour, and the bugs found were fixed. This page says which sites, what failed and why, what was fixed, and what remains. It is about whether Rampa runs and tells the truth about what it checked, not about whether its findings are right: no finding was labelled.

**In short.** Before the fixes, 37 of the 53 pages were checked as the page asked for. The other 16: 12 bot walls checked as if they were the site (9 failing with 16 findings about the vendor's page, 3 passing), one crash (Zillow), Amazon read half-parsed, and two sites that refused the browser before sending a page (X, NHK), reported in the browser library's words. After the fixes, 38 pages are checked, the 13 bot walls (Zillow's included) are reported as bot walls and exit 2, the two refusals say why in words, and nothing crashed. With `--probe all`, the share of probe kinds that could not load their page fell from 117 of 423 (28%) to 5 of 333 (1.5%), mostly through one second try on a network that dropped every 20 to 30 seconds; the probes that do run make `--probe all` take minutes on heavy pages (median 250 s, 2 pages killed at 6 minutes).

## Method

**Sites.** 53 pages, nothing behind a login ([sites.json](../../study/robustness-2026-10-23/sites.json)):

| Group | Pages |
| --- | --- |
| Brazilian government | gov.br, Receita Federal, INSS, sp.gov.br, ceara.gov.br, rj.gov.br, mg.gov.br, Câmara dos Deputados, IBGE |
| Brazilian e-commerce | Mercado Livre, Magazine Luiza, Americanas, Casas Bahia |
| Brazilian news | g1, UOL, Folha, Estadão |
| Banks (public pages) | Itaú, Banco do Brasil, Caixa, Nubank, Bradesco |
| International | Wikipedia (Web accessibility), BBC News, GitHub, MDN (HTML), Amazon, Booking.com, The New York Times, Stack Overflow (questions), Apple |
| SPAs and heavy JS | YouTube, Airbnb, Reddit, react.dev, Figma, Spotify (web player), X |
| Frames, shadow DOM, infinite scroll | W3Schools (ads in frames), Shoelace (web components), DEV Community (infinite feed) |
| Cookie walls | The Guardian, Der Spiegel, Le Monde |
| Very long pages | Wikipedia's list of countries by population, the HTML Standard's parsing chapter, WCAG 2.2 |
| Non-Latin scripts | Yahoo! JAPAN, NHK News, Al Jazeera (Arabic), an Arabic Wikipedia address |
| Known bot protection | G2, Zillow |

**Commands.** For each page, the built CLI with no model, as the task set it: `node dist/cli.mjs check <url> --no-llm --format json -o <file>` (with `--save` added, so the snapshot could be looked at), then the same with `--probe all`, and once with `--profile cognitive`. Each run had a wall-clock budget (4 minutes, 6 for `--probe all`), after which the harness killed it. Three pages ran at a time (two with `--probe all`), with `RAMPA_LOCALE=en`, `RAMPA_MODEL` empty, and `RAMPA_DEBUG=1`, so that a crash printed its stack. The harness is [run.mjs](../../study/robustness-2026-10-23/run.mjs); [summarize.mjs](../../study/robustness-2026-10-23/summarize.mjs) classifies the last run of each page and mode and writes [summary.md](../../study/robustness-2026-10-23/summary.md) (every run, both builds), `results-before.json` and `results-after.json`; [probe-loads.mjs](../../study/robustness-2026-10-23/probe-loads.mjs) counts the probe kinds that could not load their page. The reports, stderr and recordings stay out of git (`study/robustness-2026-10-23/raw/`).

**Builds.** *Before*: `main` at `d23cfb5` (0.1.1 plus the Unreleased changes). *After*: this branch, `fix/robustness`. The before runs used a copy of that build's `dist`, so the two builds could run side by side.

**The machine and its network.** Windows 11, Microsoft Edge 155 headless through playwright-core, a home connection in Brazil that dropped for a few seconds every 20 to 30 seconds for most of the day (a minute of `curl` probes: 3 failures in 12), with a steady spell in the evening. A run that failed on the network (`net::ERR_INTERNET_DISCONNECTED`, `ERR_NETWORK_CHANGED`, `ERR_NAME_NOT_RESOLVED`, or `load` not reached in 30 s) was run again with the same build, most of them in the steady spell, and the last run of each page counts; the two that failed again are counted apart, as *network*. Times are not a performance measure: they move with the network and with the other runs on the machine. The same pages run with both builds one after the other took the same time at every stage but navigation.

**Classes.** *Crash*: an uncaught error, exit 2 with a stack. *Time-out*: killed by the budget. *Collector or surface failure*: an empty or half-read snapshot, a page not loaded, a bot wall. *Probe failure*: a probe that errors or never ends. *Wrong behaviour*: a report that says something untrue about what it checked, a missing note, an advisory that fires absurdly, an impossible finding. *Expected*: a bot wall, provided Rampa says it was one.

**Live pages change.** A site may send something else from one run to the next: The New York Times sent DataDome's captcha, and its page in one run; Amazon sent a 202 interstitial, its page, and once its own captcha; Spotify's player had 2,450 elements in one run and 1,257 in another. The tables compare what kind of outcome each run had, not its findings.

## Before: what failed, and why

1. **Bot walls checked as if they were the site (12 pages, wrong behaviour).** The headless browser was answered with a challenge, a captcha or a short refusal by IBGE and Stack Overflow (Cloudflare's "Just a moment...", HTTP 403), sp.gov.br (Imperva's challenge frame, HTTP 200), rj.gov.br (a firewall's "The URL you requested has been blocked", HTTP 500), Itaú, Magazine Luiza and Casas Bahia (Akamai's "Access Denied", or the site's own page around an Akamai reference number, HTTP 403), Mercado Livre and Banco do Brasil (a short refusal page of the site's, HTTP 403, from CloudFront and Cloudflare), The New York Times and G2 (DataDome's captcha frame, HTTP 403), and Reddit ("Prove your humanity", a reCAPTCHA wall, HTTP 200). Rampa checked each of these pages as the site, in every mode: 9 exited 1 with 16 findings about the vendor's page ("Documents must have a `<title>`", "`<html>` must have a lang attribute"), and 3 exited 0, a pass for a captcha. With `--probe all`, every probe loaded the challenge again. The auth probe already recognized a bot check (`--probe auth`, not in `all`); the check itself did not.
2. **Crash on Zillow (collector, every mode).** Zillow sent HUMAN's (PerimeterX) press-and-hold captcha. Its frame is given DOM properties of its own that all read as `undefined`, `getRootNode()` and `localName` included, to keep automated browsers out of it, and the collector stopped with `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'nodeType')`. Past that, axe-core stopped on the same element (`reading 'toLowerCase'`), which would have stopped the check again.
3. **Amazon read half-parsed (collector).** amazon.com answered the headless browser with HTTP 202 and a script that sets a cookie and reloads the page. The reload began while Rampa waited for the page to settle, which ended the wait at once, and Rampa read the new document while it was still being parsed: one element, no title, and a finding that the page had no title. A page that reloads at the same address while it is being read also cost 10 s, waiting for its address to change.
4. **Busy news pages failed for an ad that kept loading (time-out).** UOL and Folha failed the cognitive run with "did not reach load within 30000 ms", on the second try too, and so did two probe kinds: the page had arrived and was drawn, but its `load` event waits for every frame and image, and an ad or a widget kept one loading.
5. **A 404 page with no word of it (wrong behaviour).** The Arabic Wikipedia address in the list names an article that does not exist; Wikipedia answered HTTP 404 with its "no such article" page, and Rampa checked that page with no note that it was an error page.
6. **Navigation errors in the browser library's words (X, NHK, and every network failure).** X answers the headless browser with HTTP 403 and an empty body, which Chromium fails as `net::ERR_HTTP_RESPONSE_CODE_FAILURE`; NHK breaks the connection off (`ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR`). Rampa exited 2, as it should, but with Playwright's message and call log, escape codes included: `rampa: page.goto: net::ERR_HTTP_RESPONSE_CODE_FAILURE at https://x.com/ Call log: [2m - navigating to …`. A navigation that failed because the network dropped for a moment failed the page, or the probe, with no second try: in the probe runs, 117 of the 423 probe kinds (28%) could not load their page, and their criteria went unchecked though the report was written.
7. **A probe failure (YouTube).** The text-spacing probe (`--probe layout`, 1.4.12) stopped with `target.style.setProperty is not a function`: YouTube's `yt-attributed-string` elements define a `style` of their own (a symbol), and the probe set the spacing through it. 1.4.12 went unchecked on YouTube. Every other probe ended normally or at its own time budget; none ran on without end.
8. **Abbreviations that are names (cognitive profile, wrong behaviour).** `coga/abbreviations` takes a token of two to eight characters with two capitals and no run of three lowercase letters for an abbreviation. Names in camel case fit that shape: TikTok (on three pages), AirTag, AdOpt, VaiVem, DevCon, FigJam and NumPy were reported as abbreviations "without an explanation", 9 of the 235 abbreviation advisories on the 48 pages the profile ran on.
9. **The cognitive profile on a page it did not read** (found after fix 1). On a bot check, the form checks said they had run on the page, so their patterns counted as screened, and the observation and presentation checks gave reasons that did not apply ("recorded without --profile cognitive").
10. **`--probe all` costs minutes, not seconds.** Each probe kind loads the page again and stops at its own time budget (the keyboard walk and hover at 120 s, orientation and shortcuts at 90 s, color at 45 s, states at 40 s). With the before build, Ceará's portal, UOL and Folha were killed at 6 minutes, and INSS, Câmara, Estadão, GitHub and Figma took more than 5. Run alone, Al Jazeera took 290 s: the keyboard walk 103 s, hover 50 s, orientation 47 s, layout 35 s. That is the cost of `--probe all` on a heavy page, not a probe that never ends, but [docs/probes.md](../probes.md) said "seconds to tens of seconds".

What held up: every page cut at the collector's 5,000 elements (gov.br, Receita Federal, INSS, Folha, Der Spiegel, the long Wikipedia list, the HTML Standard, WCAG 2.2) said so in its notes; frames of other origins and closed shadow roots were listed; pages with shadow DOM (Shoelace, MDN, YouTube), frames (W3Schools, the ad frames of news sites), cookie walls (The Guardian, Der Spiegel, Le Monde), right-to-left Arabic and Japanese were read with no error; no cognitive advisory fired absurdly often apart from the camel-case names: the most on a page was 44 abbreviations, on the Wikipedia article (WCAG, W3C, ISO, CENELEC, ETSI, PMID, ISBN...) and on UOL (TSE, STF, OAB, ANPD...), nearly all real ones.

## What was fixed

| Bug | Root cause | Fix | Regression test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bot walls checked as the site | Nothing in the check looked for them | `surfaces/bot-check.ts` recognizes a page in place of the one asked for, on a page from a server, by strong signs only: Cloudflare's `cf-mitigated: challenge` header; on a short page (under 400 elements and 3,000 characters), a vendor's challenge script, form or frame (Cloudflare, DataDome, HUMAN, Imperva, AWS WAF, Amazon's captcha), an Akamai reference number or an Imperva incident ID, a challenge page's title, or HTTP 403 or 429. A title a site's own page could have ("Just a moment", "Access denied") counts only with an HTTP error. The collector does not read such a page; the snapshot and the report record `blocked`; every format's headline says nothing was checked; the first note says what the site sent and what gave it away; stderr says so; the run exits 2; no probe and no cognitive check runs on it | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: Cloudflare's challenge, Akamai's page, DataDome's frame, a site's own short refusal (in Portuguese), a page titled "Just a moment..." and a local file titled "Prove your humanity" that are checked as usual, the headline in pretty, Markdown and HTML, the cognitive profile, the CLI's exit code and stderr, and the rules for telling a bot check from a page |
| Crash on a masked element | The collector read the element's own `getRootNode()`, which gave `undefined`; axe-core then stopped on it too | An element whose `localName` or `nodeType` is not what the DOM gives is left out with what it holds, and counted (`reach.masked`, with a note); a `getRootNode()` that gives nothing no longer breaks a ref. When axe-core stops with an error, the rest of the check runs and a note gives the error (`error` in the engine results) | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: a page that masks a frame as HUMAN's captcha does, and an element with only `getRootNode()` replaced. On the old collector it fails with Zillow's `TypeError` |
| Amazon read half-parsed | The wait after load ended when the page navigated, and the collector read whatever document was there | After the wait, a page whose document was replaced, or is not loaded yet, is waited for (its `readyState`, at most 15 s) and settles again; a page that navigates while it is read waits for its new document instead of for a new address | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: an interstitial that sets a cookie and reloads into a page whose body arrives after the wait gives up; it fails with the old wait |
| Busy news pages failed on `load` | `openPage` failed the page when `load` did not fire within `--timeout` | When the document arrived and was parsed but `load` has not fired by the timeout, the page is read as it is and a note says that what was still loading may be missing (`reach.loadTimedOutMs`). A page whose document never arrives still fails, and so does `--wait-for networkidle` or `domcontentloaded` | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: a page with an image that never arrives, and one that never answers |
| 404 page with no note | The status of the page's response was not kept | The snapshot and the report keep `httpStatus` (400 or more), and the first note says the page checked came with that status | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: a 404 page is checked, with the note, and exits as usual |
| Navigation errors in Playwright's words; no second try | `openPage` passed Playwright's error through | A browser network error is said in words, without the call log ("could not be loaded (net::ERR_HTTP_RESPONSE_CODE_FAILURE): the server answered with an HTTP error (403) and an empty page, as some bot protection does with automated browsers"), and a navigation that fails because the network dropped or the connection was cut is tried once more after 2 s, the second error saying why when it differs; the probes open their pages the same way | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: a closed port, an empty 403, a server that cuts two connections (Chromium tries once more by itself), and one that cuts them and then answers an empty 403 |
| Text-spacing probe failed on YouTube | `applyTextSpacing` set the spacing through `el.style`, which a custom element can redefine | The spacing goes through the browser's own inline style (the `style` getter of `HTMLElement` and `SVGElement`) | `test/robustness-web.test.ts`: a custom element whose class defines `style`; it fails with YouTube's error on the old probe |
| Camel-case names as abbreviations | The candidate shape let two capitalized syllables through | A token made of two or more capitalized parts (TikTok, AirTag) is a name; abbreviations that end in capitals (PhD) are still read | `test/advisory-unit.test.ts`; it fails without the fix |
| The cognitive profile on a page it did not read | The profile ran its checks on the empty tree | On a bot check or an XML document every check says it did not run, and why | `test/robustness-web.test.ts` |

## After

| Mode | Before: checked as the page asked for | Before: other outcomes | After: checked | After: other outcomes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default | 38, Amazon's half-read report among them | 12 bot walls checked as the site, 1 crash, 2 refusals in Playwright's words | 38 | 13 bot walls reported, 2 refusals said in words |
| `--probe all` | 35 | 12 bot walls checked as the site, 3 killed at 6 min, 1 crash, 2 refusals | 37 | 12 bot walls reported (The New York Times sent its page this time), 2 killed at 6 min (g1, Estadão), 2 refusals |
| `--profile cognitive` | 36 | 12 bot walls checked as the site, 2 load time-outs, 1 crash, 2 refusals | 36 | 13 bot walls reported (Amazon sent its captcha this time), 2 network failures after the second try (rj.gov.br, Spotify), 2 refusals |

- **Crashes:** 3 (Zillow, in each mode) before, none after.
- **Bot walls:** 12 checked as the site before, every one reported after, in every mode, and no other page reported as one. Zillow is now the 13th: its captcha, which crashed the collector, is reported as HUMAN's bot check; Amazon's captcha, the one run it was sent, was reported as Amazon's.
- **Probe kinds that could not load their page:** 117 of 423 before (28%: 114 on the network, 2 on `load`, and YouTube's text-spacing error), 5 of 333 after (1.5%, all on the network, after the second try). Before, a report written with most of its probes failed counted as checked; the before build's probe runs look faster for that reason (on the pages that were not bot walls, a median of 157 s against 250 s).
- **Load time-outs:** Folha's and Estadão's pages are read with the note in the runs where `load` never fired (Folha in every mode, Estadão in the cognitive run).
- **The 404 page** gets its note; **X and NHK** say what the site did: "could not be loaded (net::ERR_HTTP_RESPONSE_CODE_FAILURE): the server answered with an HTTP error (403) and an empty page, as some bot protection does with automated browsers", "could not be loaded (net::ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR, on a second try too): the server broke the connection off, as some bot protection does with automated browsers".
- **Abbreviations:** 217 advisories before and 191 after on the 34 pages both builds read with the profile (the pages change between runs too); no camel-case name is among those after.
- **Time with `--probe all`:** median 250 s on the 37 pages checked; 9 of them took more than 5 minutes (Folha, BBC News, Figma and W3Schools close to 6), and g1 and Estadão were killed at 6. In default mode the median page took 10 s, with the cognitive profile 30 s.

## What remains

- **Rampa's headless browser is refused by 15 of the 53 sites** (the 13 bot walls, X, and NHK at the connection), and by Amazon at times. Rampa now says so instead of checking the vendor's page. It does not disguise its browser or solve challenges: a site's owner can let the machine that runs Rampa through, send a header the protection accepts with `--header`, or check a copy without the protection.
- **`--probe all` takes minutes on heavy pages**, each kind bounded by its own time budget (they add up to more than 8 minutes, page loads aside); [docs/probes.md](../probes.md) now gives the measured cost. There is no budget for all the probes together.
- **One page that cannot be loaded ends a run of several targets** with exit 2 and no report for the others. This study ran one target per run, so it did not meet this; a crawl (`--crawl`) already lists such a page as not checked and goes on.
- **One second try is not always enough.** When the connection dropped for several seconds at a time, some runs failed twice; they are counted as network.
- **Words in capitals that the page's dictionary does not know are still taken for abbreviations**: English words on a Portuguese page (EMAIL, HOST, RECORD on UOL), Spanish words in an English reference ("BOLETIN OFICIAL" on Wikipedia), names in capitals (VAN DEN BROEK). Telling them apart needs the language of each run of text, which pages rarely mark.
- **Other in-page code sets inline styles through `el.style`** (contrast, image and label captures, the color, focus and hover probes), so a custom element that redefines `style` could still break one of those steps where it is the element styled; only the text-spacing probe, which styles every element, met one here.
