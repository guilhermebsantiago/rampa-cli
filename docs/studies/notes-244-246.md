# 2.4.4 and 2.4.6: what the model claimed on the dev pages, and what Rampa reports now

The [real-page study](real-pages-2026-10.md) found no real failure among the judgment layer's findings for 2.4.4 (Link Purpose, In Context) and 2.4.6 (Headings and Labels): 0 of 55 and 0 of 45 in the first run, and 0 again in the [re-measurement on its test pages](real-pages-2026-10-remeasure.md). On the W3C ACT test cases the same criteria score well. These notes take the study's **dev split only**, read every 2.4.4 and 2.4.6 finding in it, ask what fact would have refuted each one, compare ways of reporting these criteria, and explain the one Rampa now uses.

**In short.** On the dev pages the model made 38 claims about links and headings, and none was a real failure. The ones today's verification still lets through are readings: of a specific text ("ISTs", "Download", "Introduction"), of whether a context completes a generic one ("Clique aqui" in a sentence that says what it leads to), or of a destination. No fact Rampa can check tells them apart from real failures: lexical facts such as "the heading shares no word with its section" are true of a third of the real headings on these pages. **The honest answer is that this model cannot judge specific link and heading texts reliably on real pages.** So a model's fail on 2.4.4 or 2.4.6 is now a finding only when a fact Rampa checks itself supports it: a generic text such as "Read more" with no context of its own, a raw address, links that share a text and lead elsewhere from the same context, a numbered heading under no subject, or a label several fields share with nothing to tell them apart. Every other fail is reported as the model's *suggestion*, in the report's items to review, never as a failure, and the criterion's coverage says it needs review. Of the 38 dev claims, none is a finding now (19 were still reported at low confidence before, 15 of them false positives and 4 debatable, all of which are now suggestions). On the ACT test cases and pairs, the default threshold now catches failures main hid below it (fd3a94's identical links in one context, cc0f0a's shared labels, the generic link pairs), with two false positives on fd3a94 passed examples that ACT and Rampa already read differently; the failures no fact supports, such as "Weather" over opening hours, are suggestions, and counting them gives back main's numbers with low confidence counted.

### What the claims have in common

- **Half were already handled.** Verification since the study drops 17 of the 38 (the address-only, uninformative-title and broken-link mismatches, and the numbered series), and judging the visible label removes 2 more.
- **What is left is reading, not fact.** Of the 19 left, 4 are the debatable "More..." links and headings. The other 15, all false positives, are the model's reading of a specific text ("ISTs", "Download", "Introduction", "DESTAQUE") or of a destination ("Declaração de Cookies"). In each, the judges refuted the claim by reading WCAG's intent: the context counts (sentence, list, section), short headings are fine, a broader destination that contains what the text names is not a mismatch. Nothing Rampa can compute from the snapshot separates these from real failures, because on these pages there were no real failures to separate them from.
- **More context did not fix it.** Most of the "says nothing" link claims were made with the link's sentence in the prompt ("Web site source code is available."). More context in the prompts was added before the study's re-measurement (`2239c30`, `c7fe9e3`), and the held-out claims stayed at 0 true.
- **The model's confidence says nothing.** All 38 were "high".

## What Rampa does now

<!-- built:start -->
Option b with the refinements of d (commits `d6ebe8a` and `ab2d4dc`):

- **The hook.** A criterion can say whether a verified fail is `grounded` on a fact it checks. 2.4.4 and 2.4.6 do. A grounded fail is a finding with the model's confidence. Any other verified fail goes to the report's `needsReview` as the model's suggestion: the claim as the message, the model's suggested text as the evidence, and the model's name. It never fails a build, never sits below the threshold, and has no fingerprint to waive. The coverage counts it on the judgment method as review, with its own count ("1 of them model suggestions with no checked fact behind them"), so the criterion's status is "needs review", not "no failure found". `rampa eval` records the suggestions per page and counts them in its summary; they do not fail a page. The `--no-verify` ablation keeps every claim as a finding, as before.
- **2.4.4's facts.** `generic`: the text is generic and the link has no programmatically determined context of its own (no sentence, paragraph, list item or cell, header cells or description), so nothing can complete it. A generic text is one on the list, or one made only of words that say "follow me" (a verb that names no destination, such as "click", "see", "read", "check", "veja", "confira", with a word that only points, such as "here", "this", "it", "more", "aqui"): "Check it out", "Veja aqui", "Clique aqui para ver mais". On the dev pages this word rule added nothing beyond the list; the link pairs' "Check it out" is such a phrasing, so those pairs now measure this rule rather than the model (see the limits). `url_or_filename`: an address with a path or a query, or a file name; a bare host such as "ufc.br" names its site. `ambiguous`: the link-set fact verification already required. `mismatch`: never.
- **Sections as context.** Links that share a text are compared with the headings of the sections they sit in, not only the nearest one. On MDN's page, 25 "Play" buttons sit each under a "Result" or "HTML" heading of its own example: with the nearest heading, 14 were an "ambiguous" set; with the sections, none is. ACT fd3a94's failed examples have no nested sections, so they are unchanged.
- **2.4.6's facts.** `generic` and `placeholder_text`: a text on the generic lists; a heading that is a number with at most one word in a numbered series whose parent names no subject; a label several fields share with no visible heading or group to tell them apart. `mismatch`: never.
- **The cap.** On a long page the model judges first the candidates a fact could ground, then the rest in page order.
- **Versions.** 2.4.4 is version 3 and 2.4.6 version 4. Their prompts did not change, so the demo's 15 recorded answers were moved to the new keys rather than asked again, and the measurements above replay offline.

## Limits

<!-- limits:start -->
- **No true failure to keep on the dev pages.** The dev split has no true positive for either criterion, so these notes can show what each option stops reporting there, not what real failures it keeps. The four uncertain "More..." findings stand in for that, and the ACT test cases and pairs measure what is kept on purpose-built failures. Real failures of these criteria on real pages are rare; the next measurement on fresh pages should label a sample of the suggestions too, to estimate what a person would find in them.
- **Context read generously.** The rule "a generic text with no context of its own" uses the context Rampa gives the model, which counts a short block around a link as its sentence. debian.org's "More..." links sit in such a block with a describing paragraph, so the model's claim on them is a suggestion; the study's auditor counted them as failures (F63: the paragraph is not tied to the link). This errs toward fewer findings.
- **The facts are narrow on purpose.** A heading or label that names the wrong thing (ACT b49b2e's "Weather" over opening hours, cc0f0a's "Menu" on a first-name field), a link whose text promises another page, and a specific link text that really says nothing (5effbb's "Workshop" with its description in another paragraph) are only suggestions now. Rampa does not detect them as failures, and says so in the coverage ("needs review").
- **The generic lists are hand-written.** They cover English, Portuguese and Spanish. The "follow me" word rule was written after reading the corrupted pairs, whose generic text is "Check it out", so the 2.4.4 `link-generic` pairs now measure the rule, not the model's generalization; on the dev pages it added no candidate.
- **One reading of "context".** The sections a link sits in count when Rampa compares links with one text, as the heading above a link already did. WCAG 2.2's sufficient techniques for 2.4.4 list the sentence, paragraph, list item and table cell; the preceding heading is an advisory technique (H80). A strict reader of ACT fd3a94 could count MDN's "Play" buttons as failures.
- **Labels from Claude agents.** The dev labels are the study's two Claude judges, a pre-annotation; the new labels in these notes are one Claude agent's. Both need human review.
- **One model, one run.** Gemma 4 12B through Ollama, temperature 0, on a GPU other jobs shared. A larger model may read specific texts better; nothing here measured one, and the gate does not depend on the model.

## Data and reproduction

- `study/notes-244-246/run.sh` rebuilds every number above, from the repository root after `pnpm build`: `collect` saves the twelve dev pages with their link destinations and no model call, `facts` computes the facts of every candidate and scores the 38 labelled claims under each option (`dev-facts.ts`, `groundable.ts`, `dev_options.py`), `act` runs `rampa eval` on 2.4.4 and 2.4.6 and scores each option (`options.py`), and `dev` judges the saved dev pages (`dev_judged.py`).
- Snapshots, judgment caches and run outputs go to `.rampa/`, which is kept out of git, as in the study. The pages change, so a new `collect` gives somewhat different candidates than the snapshots of 2026-10-11 used here.
- The runs here: Gemma 4 12B through Ollama on 2026-10-11, one judgment per candidate, 150 judgments on the ACT pages and pairs and 223 on the dev pages. Since the prompts are the same under every option and before and after this change, each option is the same answers scored differently, and a run replays offline from its cache.
