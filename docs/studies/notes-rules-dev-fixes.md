# Rules and collector fixes from the fresh-page study's dev split

The [fresh-page study](real-pages-2026-10-fresh.md) listed, from its **dev split only**, three problems outside the judged criteria: `rampa/link-color-only` (1.4.1) wrong 10 times out of 10, `rampa/focus-visible` (2.4.7) and `rampa/focus-indicator-contrast` (1.4.11) reporting text fields whose indicator is the caret, and a page cut at the collector's 5,000 elements with nothing in the report. Rampa Lab's own check added a fourth: `rampa/placeholder-text` (2.4.6) took the Lab's filter label "Your label" for a template's text. These notes say what changed and what each change did on the dev pages.

**Data used.** The twelve dev pages of the study: their saved recordings (24 files, plus 8 of the probe run, all matching the SHA-256 in `study/fresh-2026-10-11/data/recordings.json`), their labels in `data/findings.json` (rows with `split: "dev"`, read with `scripts/dump_fp.py dev`), and the four dev pages of the probe subset loaded again live. **The test split was not used:** no test page was loaded, replayed or read, and no test label or false positive was read. The labels are the study's two Claude judges', a pre-annotation, not ground truth.

## What changed

1. **`rampa/link-color-only` compares a link with the text around it** (color probe version 2). The probe compared each link with its block's own computed color and type. On medlineplus.gov the block is a list item whose computed color, #444444, colors no visible text: the only text next to each title link is a 12 px, #666666 source note. Now the probe reads the block's own visible text outside links and controls, groups it by how it looks, and compares the link with the group that has most of it: its color, and its size, weight, style, family and underline as cues. A block with less than 20 characters of such text (a list of links, a menu, a row of links with separators) holds no link in text, so the rule does not apply to its links. The record quotes the text compared with (`aroundText`), and the finding's evidence quotes it too. Version 1 records are read as before. WCAG's basis is F73 and G183; the W3C ACT rules have no rule for links in text, so there are no ACT test cases for this rule.
2. **A text field's caret counts as its focus indicator.** WCAG's Understanding for 2.4.7 gives it as an example: "When text fields receive focus, a vertical bar is displayed in the field, indicating that the user can insert text, OR all of the text is highlighted, indicating that the user can type over the text." The Understanding for 1.4.11 passes a text input whose other indicator is under 3:1 because "the cursor/caret which is displayed when the input receives focus does provide a sufficiently strong visual indication" (Figure 33), and fails a custom caret under 3:1 that is the only indication (Figure 44). The keyboard walk's captures hide the caret, which blinks, so the probe now reads it from the field's styles (`caret` on a stop's `focus`: `caret-color`, or the text color, on the field's background). `rampa/focus-visible` no longer reports a text field (a text-like input, a textarea, editable content) whose caret shows, and still reports buttons, links, non-text inputs, read-only fields and a caret made invisible. `rampa/focus-indicator-contrast` does not judge a text field whose caret has 3:1 against the field. A recording made before the probe read carets assumes the browser's default caret on what the snapshot names a text field, for 2.4.7 only, and the coverage note says how many.
3. **A page cut at the collector's limit says so.** The snapshot already recorded `truncated`; it now also records `elementLimit`, and every report says, among its notes, that the page was cut at that many elements, that Rampa's rules and judged criteria did not run past the cut (naming the criteria they checked only up to it), that a probe's findings past it cannot be pointed at, and that axe-core read the whole page: pretty, Markdown, HTML, JSON (`coverage.truncated`), SARIF (the page's coverage properties and its coverage notification) and the MCP tool, in English and Portuguese.
4. **`rampa/placeholder-text` matches only labels no page means.** "Your label" was on its list of template labels. In Rampa Lab it names a filter by the label a reviewer gave each finding, a real label. The list now holds only labels that tell someone to put a label there ("Label here", "Your label here", "Seu rótulo aqui"), matched exactly, besides lorem ipsum and a word with a number ("Field 1"). "Your label", "Field label", "Input label", "Label text", "Seu rótulo" and "Texto do rótulo" left the list: each can name a field that is about a label.

## Effect on the dev pages

**The saved recordings, replayed.** The 12 recordings of the dev pages and the 4 of the probe run (a snapshot and its engine results each), checked offline with each build. This holds the pages fixed, but the recordings were made by the old probes: they have no caret and compare links the old way.

**Rampa Lab.** The filter that `rampa/placeholder-text` flagged was renamed in the Lab before its review mode was committed ("Labeled as"), so the Lab no longer has it. A copy of that filter's markup (`<label for="filter-status">Your label</label>` on a `select` of label values) gave one `rampa/placeholder-text` finding with the old build and none with the new one. `test/fixtures/rules/content-pass.html` now holds it.

## ACT test cases and fixtures

- **`rampa/link-color-only`.** The W3C ACT rules have no rule for links told apart by color: in the ACT test cases Rampa reads, no rule maps to 1.4.1, so this rule has no ACT test cases. Its fixtures: `color-fail.html`, `color-pass.html` and `color-near.html` give the same results as before. Two new ones: `color-links-fail.html` (a paragraph's unstyled link, and a link in a block whose own color is the link's while its words are gray) gives 2 findings, where the old probe gave 1 and called the second "styled like the text around it"; `color-links-pass.html` (a menu, a resource list with a smaller note, a row of links with separators, an underlined link) gives none, where the old probe gave 2, the resource list's.
- **`rampa/focus-visible`.** ACT `oj04fd`, its 9 test cases run with `--probe keyboard` by both builds: the same results (Failed Example 1 failed, high; nothing on the passed and inapplicable examples). None of them has a text field. `focus-fail.html`, `focus-pass.html` and `focus-obscured.html` give the same results. The new `focus-caret.html` gives 6 findings where the old build gave 10: a search field, a textarea and an editable region with no focus style but their caret are no longer reported, nor (for 1.4.11) a field whose faint ring is under 3:1 and whose default caret has 18.88:1; a field with `caret-color: transparent`, a read-only field, a submit input, a button and a link with no focus style are still reported, and so is a field whose only indicator besides a faint ring is a pale caret (#00c7fd, 1.98:1, Figure 44).
- **Demo replay.** `node .rampa/misses.ts` on each of the six `demo/recorded/*.snapshot.json` reports no miss.
