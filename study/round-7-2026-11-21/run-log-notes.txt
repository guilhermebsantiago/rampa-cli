12:00  W3C documents for the rubric, read on w3.org (curl, text extracted locally): ACT rules fd3a94 (proposed; maps to
       2.4.4 Level A, 2.4.9 secondary) and b20e66 (proposed; maps to 2.4.9 only), the ACT rules index ("Proposed rules
       ... have not yet been approved by the relevant Working Groups. ACT Rules are informative"), Understanding 2.4.4
       and 1.1.1 (WCAG 2.1), techniques H30, H33, G91, F89, H2, G82, F63, G94. Decisions (judge-rubric.md):
       R1: 2.4.4 shared-text claims are scored by fd3a94's expectation (same resource, equivalent resources, or nothing
           visible tells them apart); the set is formed with the rubric's context list (the sixth study's), not ACT's
           glossary, so the context rule does not change; every link of a failing pair fails, including the correctly
           named one when the other link carries the wrong name (the rule's outcome is the set's, and its assumption says
           at least one of the names misdescribes); the rationale names the wrong one.
       R2: an image link whose alternative describes the picture: first, does the alternative itself tell where the link
           goes (G82's test; Understanding 2.4.4 allows other words than the document's name)? If not: for 2.4.4 a title
           that with the alternative gives the purpose makes it pass (H33 is sufficient, its test quoted), and a
           neighbouring text link to the same address only when it sits in the listed context (F63, F89's second
           paragraph, H2 advisory); for 1.1.1 neither does (1.1.1's control clause, H30 under Situation C, the "linked
           thumbnail" example: the image's alternative must identify the link's function; a title is the link's
           description, not the image's alternative or the link's name).
       The rubric also says that where it fixes a reading (2.4.4 context, R1, R2), auditors' possible disagreement on
       that point is not a reason for "uncertain".
